# `photo`

The rest of the commands are in [`COMMANDS.md`](../COMMANDS.md).

Photo mode as a player uses it (`page/photo.ts`, `src/ui/photo.ts`), in the village at dusk, then from the village's pause menu, the Thornwood and the crypt, each over the shoulder. In each place: opened by P (on, the solo game paused, the grade's look on); every element of the page outside photo mode's panel and the game's canvas that a player would see (a box, visible, opaque, with text or a background of its own) listed, none allowed (the pad covers the screen, so what's under it can't be found by a point); the world's overlays drawn in no frame (`overFrame.draw` counted with the frames drawn); the game's clock and the hero still over 30 frames; the camera flown at least 1 m by W in 30 frames and turned 1 rad by a 200 px drag on its pad (within 0.05); the contrast slider and the Cinematic filter setting the grade's uniforms, drawn, with no program compiled; the time of day settable in the village and greyed out in a battleground; Hide hero putting every mesh of his on the hidden layer and back; a photo by Space caught as the page hands it to the browser (the download itself left out): named `last-stand-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMMSS.png`, at the canvas's pixels, its middle lit, and once letterboxed its bar's row black. Then a shot is set up (the letterbox, a filter, the vignette, the hour in the village, the panel hidden) and pictured, a photo taken of it, and Esc must turn it off, the grade's look and vignette the game's, the page's UI back and the game going on; opened from the pause menu's button, Esc must bring the pause menu back. Pictured as it opens and with the shot set up, in each place (`out/photo`). `--canary` takes `body.photo` off and draws the overlays again, each to be caught.
