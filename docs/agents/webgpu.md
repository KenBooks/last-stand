# The WebGPU renderer

From `src/core/AGENTS.md`: three's WebGPU renderer as it drew the scene (the trial behind `?renderer=webgpu`, `&meshlets=1`) and what each of WebGL's patches had on its side. Paths are under `src/core/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

**The Rust engine draws the game now, and nothing else does** (`src/engine/AGENTS.md`, `src/engine/STATE.md`): three's WebGL path and three's WebGPU drawing of the scene were taken out, the address reads no `?renderer=`, `&meshlets=1` or `&lights=each`, and the lab has no `--renderer`. three's WebGPURenderer is still made, as the engine's host (the labels' and the outline's canvas over the engine's), and the code named below is gone or going from `webgpu.ts` and `shaderPorts.ts` (the meshlets' with it): grep for a name before relying on it. The notes below are history, kept for what was measured and for what three's renderer does to anything it's still asked to draw; none of their commands with a renderer in them runs as written. A material's own look is the engine's to draw now: a new one brings its look as WGSL (`rustLook`, `src/engine/looks/`), where it got a port for three here.

- `webgpu.ts`, `rendererChoice.ts`, `shaderPorts.ts`, `meshlets.ts` (the GPU-driven drawing's clusters made in workers, `meshlets.worker.ts`): three's WebGPU renderer, which drew the scene as a trial behind `?renderer=webgpu` before the engine (`&meshlets=1` its GPU-driven drawing,
which only the address asked for: on a phone the hero stood stuck mid pose under them; `docs/research/webgpu.md`); a GLSL `ShaderMaterial` is drawn there by its port (`portShader`: its shader in WGSL, its uniforms read off the material drawn), so a new one gets a port, or a boot logs it "Not ported to WebGPU". A ported or look-given material carries a node of its own (`refreshedEachDraw`): three refreshes a still mesh's uniforms only when the material it was given holds a node, and without one the outline's strength stayed at the warm-up's 0. A kit material (`materials.ts`) is refreshed as a plain one is, and also when its looks change (`stillWhereStill`, `looksChanged`: kept on each render object, as three's observer is one for every render object of a node build, and kept on it the two dummies' looks took turns and refreshed every draw): refreshed at every draw, the lobby's two training dummies were 47 full refreshes a frame, standing still. `?builds`' `__webgpuFull` names each full refresh's cause (a node, animated, its looks, moved, changed). A still material (`stillNodes`: see-through, kit) still carries its node, made still at every draw (three marks a build as holding a node again as each of its render objects is first drawn): the meshlets' pipeline draws a copy of each material, which takes the node with it, and a copy without one drew its batches as they first drew, so once the clusters' pool grew a few seconds in, the forest's standing stones and boulders were drawn nowhere (3455227 to 1c2cfbe). That copy takes every own property, a `customProgramCacheKey` too (`seeThrough` sets one, for WebGL), and three keyed the copy's node build by it alone, so batches of one see-through material shared the first one's build and its offsets into the culling's buffers: the crypt's obelisks and beacons drew another batch's range, nothing at all, whenever their batch built second (a race with the warm-ups, so a picture or two proved nothing). Each batch's draws were then built apart (`batchesBuiltApart`, taken out with the meshlets' drawing, as `batchesKept` and `batchMoved` below); the stones likely went the same way. Found with stalls' `--before` (a change made just before the picture) and `--read` of the batch's uniforms against its range. Built apart, each batch is a full node build (about 6.5 ms), so a batch made at a draw holds that frame up: three dropped every batch not drawn in the projection that resized its render list's culling (each warm-up's new biome, the lobby's frames shrinking it to their own) and any left undrawn for 60 projections, so the lobby's frames, the warm-ups' draws and the fight's first frames made theirs again, 1477 builds at a draw in the lobby's first seconds, frames held up 200 to 740 ms. `batchesKept` keeps them (a resized culling sized for the undrawn too, which are moved into it) and the scene pass is sized before the warm-ups compile into it (its culling is made for the size drawn at: compiled at 1 px, the lobby's first frame made all 35 again). A lobby that stays (`--warm-behind`) no longer holds up past about 100 ms on a return, and Start once it's warm makes no batch, only about 40 to 80 ms of the crowds' shadows' builds. The scene's matrix walk is no lever: all of it is 0.22 ms a frame (804 objects), hidden biomes or not. A batch's draws are refreshed only as the batch moves in its culling's buffers or any buffer is freed (`batchMoved`): those were 38 full refreshes a frame changing nothing, but refreshed on a relocate alone, whole passes were refused every frame (`Buffer used in submit while destroyed`, 500 times a boot; a boot's `page errors` say so). Of three's per-object time about half is the full refresh (bindings 2.1 ms, nodes 1.3, under the profiler); what was left of it in the lobby was mostly the hero's 68 scene draws, each moved part refreshed whole for its matrix, now drawn as one (`models/drawAsOne.ts`). A render bundle still asks each of its objects whether to refresh (its draws cost nothing, its `needsRefresh` does), so a moving object in a bundle saves little: it needs its matrices elsewhere first. three executes a pass's bundles as it ends, after what's blended, so opaque meshes in one would draw over the glows before them: `bundlesBeforeTransparents` executes them before the first blended draw. A stand-in's refresh looks at its base's values alone (`baseChanged`): three's observer compared 85 properties a draw, through accessors. A texture read only by still meshes isn't sent by their refresh: what changes it sends it (`uploadTexture`). `?builds`' `window.__meshletBatches` says of each batch whether a compile or a draw made it, at what size, in which render list and culling, and what became of the one it replaced. A pipeline first needed at a draw is made beside the frames, its object left out till then (`pipelinesBeside`), so anything drawn only once into a texture (the still shadows, the environment maps) draws inside `pipelinesAtOnce`, or it would miss what wasn't made yet.
- **Each WebGPU frame begins a node frame of its own** (`newNodeFrame` in `renderWebGPU`): three's passes draw their scene once a node frame, and only three's own animation loop began one, at the browser's frames. A frame drawn without a new one drew the post chain and the overlays over the scene's last picture: a phone's game went on with the scene stuck (from play: "everything stuck, but not frozen"), and main's lobby here, three's loop held, changed 9% of the screen's middle as the hero walked (the labels), 95% now (`lab profile --renderer meshlets` held it).
- **What WebGL's GLSL patches do has a WebGPU side of its own:** the scene's MSAA, chosen once as the game starts (`setSceneSamples`, `?msaa=N`: a pipeline is made for one count), and its share of the screen (`setSceneShare`); bloom's levels weighed as `BLOOM_LEVELS` by its tints; the environment shadowed (`shadeEnvironmentNodes`: the sun's shadow in a variable, read by the direct light and by the terms the lights leave). A uniform node set once a render goes in `renderGroup`: in the object's group, the default, a still material's shared refresh never sends it, and see-through props stayed at the warm-up's, solid before the hero. The GPU-driven drawing makes its draws' materials from a material's own enumerable properties and never converts it, so what `takePorts` sets on a converted one misses it: a still see-through material holds its mask itself (`maskOwn`). A kit's `colorNode` keeps its map's alpha: taken from the opacity alone, the mage's ink hid his forearm.
- **The lamps' loop on WebGPU lists only the lamps that can light the view** (`makeLampsNode` in `core/lampNodes.ts`,
  WebGL's loop in `core/lamps.ts`): every lit pixel looped over all 24 lamps, read each one's light, and turned each
  lamp's place into the view by a matrix product, lit or not (by day and in a fight's arena every lamp is out). Its
  uniforms are packed anew at each render for its camera (`update(frame)`): the lamps lit whose reach meets the
  view's four sides, each with its place already in the view's space, its place in the world and its index for its
  shadows' tiles, and how many there are; the loop stops after the last, and tests the distance before it reads the
  light. The same pixels by day and by night (`lab passes --screen 832x384@3.75 --boot verylow
  --village --hour 12|0 --ab`); at a phone's pixels (`--device phone`) the village's scene pass by day 15.7 ms to
  11.4 (four runs a side, the old `lampNodes.ts` swapped in), by night the same, about 24: lit and in view, a
  lamp's cost is its lighting, not its being found. An hour held matters to any village A/B: the page's own moves the
  sun between the sides, and its shadows differed by 5% of the pixels.
- **The village's sight is worked out in the final pass** (`seenOnly` in `webgpu.ts`, WebGL's `SIGHT_GLSL` in
  `core/sight.ts`): two rows of nine lookups a pixel across the rays, two `atan`s, the roofs' map, at the scene's
  full size. Its port worked all of it out with the fog off too (a fight's, a whole view's) and weighed it by nothing,
  where WebGL's returns at once: the lab's crypt at a phone's pixels, its final pass 4.4 ms to 0.15
  (`lab passes --screen 832x384@3.75 --boot verylow --ab`, the pictures the same). It's a `select`
  on the fog's being on now, as the doorway's row is on a door seen through; and built plain (`plainAtBoot`, Low
  and Very low at the boot) it takes 3 taps across the rays where 9 (`PLAIN_SIGHT_TAPS`): in the village at Very low
  1.59 ms to 0.75 (`lab passes --village`), the fog's edge as soft to the eye. From the owner's phone in the village
  at Very low (trace FJJ1-Z9XM): the post chain 6.5 ms of a 17 ms frame before its GPU slowed.
- **WebGPU's frame is about its CPU work** (the forest lobby at 1518x768: GPU 2.47 ms, CPU 8.05, the perf overlay's). What's cut: a crowd's materials are still (`stillNodes`, their rows sent by `writeRows`, once a frame: a shadow's render of the scene wrote them again; a texture made anew refreshes its slots in full for a frame, `refreshedInFull`, or their bindings would hold the old one); so is the grass (its clock in `renderGroup`); culled instances stay static in usage (three refreshes dynamic ones in full at every draw, and a new version already says they changed); the sun's shadow draws every other frame from about 65 frames a second by the frames' mean (`sunShadowInTurns`, `?shadowEvery=1`: by each frame's own time, one that missed the screen's refresh had the next draw the shadow too, and the game stuck at half the screen's rate); and one of the adaptation's three grids a frame. Left, under the profiler: the meshlets' `project` and `finishRenderList` about 1.2 ms, the hero's pose 1.4 (WebGL's too), the post chain's 15 renders about 0.8.
