# The Ossuary Twins' models

`src/entities/models/twins.ts` and `ossuary.ts` (their fight: `src/entities/twins.ts`, `twinSkulls.ts`). Moved out of
`src/entities/models/AGENTS.md` as it passed 60k characters; read it before changing either model.

## Their bodies

The Ossuary Twins, about 5.5 m tall, every bone sculpted by `ossuary.ts`: each bone a distance field meshed with surface nets at the size it's drawn, its hollows darkened in its vertex colours (`netGeometry` in `world/skull.ts`), so its ends read: a femur's ball, trochanter and condyles, a tibia's plateau with its fibula beside it, a humerus's head and elbow, the ulna's hook and the radius crossing to the thumb, a pelvis of flared wings with its sockets cut and its rings open, shoulder blades with their spines, a breastbone; the ribs, vertebrae, fingers and toes are tubes carrying vertex colours too, so all of a twin's bone is one material (`toned`), the cartilage a darker shade of it. From Kenneth's look at the first models ("we need better models", "improve face": ribs as identical rings round a post, a ball for a head, balls for skulls): each face is the crypt's skull (`world/skull.ts`) with parts of its own (`twinSkull`: Slab's `heavy`, a thick brow and cheekbones, a crack across the crown, a tooth knocked out; Reliquary's `long`, its crown drawn back, its cheeks hollow, a third socket in its brow with a glow in it) and a jaw sculpted to fit it, hinged at its condyles (`JAW_HINGE`); its eyes small glows deep in the sockets (as large as the socket, the glow filled it, a disc where the hollow should read), its neck's vertebrae well behind the jaw (under the skull's middle, the top one showed in the jaw's curve from above). Its ribs curve from their vertebrae round their angle to a breastbone on their cartilage, the next two to the cartilage above, the last floating. Slab broad, his bones thick, iron pauldrons of three riveted lames, bracers and knee cops (a pale, less metallic iron: a darker one drew black in the crypt's night, with no sky to mirror), a yoke of five skulls across his upper back, a shawl off one shoulder, a loincloth of layered aprons and tongues (`drape`: a panel on an arc, laid in folds, its hem torn into tongues with slits between some), a tombstone extruded with chamfered edges, its shoulder chipped, its foot broken off, carved with a ringed cross; his chain of bone his model's own instanced mesh of added light from the ring on his belt, laid by the game each frame, sagging slack and drawn straight and brighter as it nears its reach. Reliquary thin, hooded (a cowl peaked behind and open before its face), a capelet, a robe open over its legs, torn sleeves, prayer beads of vertebrae, a bronze reliquary with a skull behind its grille, five skulls on a turning ring round it; its hands on the casket's sides wherever it's held (`reach`, a two-bone reach to a pole: with its arms swung free, its bones showed its hands held out at its sides). A man's skull and jaw are meshed once a grid and shared by both twins and every small skull (`kept`). Each is at most 45k triangles, as a phone's budget for a boss: 42k and 44k, first 69k and 76k, cut by meshing each bone on a coarser grid (the shading is the field's own gradient, so it stays smooth) and by fewer, simpler knuckles; `lab boss --parts twinsLook` pictures each from every side and lists where its triangles go. `mirrored` mirrors a bone with its vertex colours: `shapes.ts`'s `mirrorX` leaves them where they were

## Their belts

`twins.ts`: each twin's belt (`belts`): a rope of `BELT_SKULLS` skulls at its hip on its throwing side (Slab's left, his right holding the tombstone; Reliquary's right, which lets go of the casket to throw), its own kind's in front and its brother's behind, its rope hidden at a scale of none till it inherits them (drawn at that scale, so its pipelines are made with the warm-up), and a skull of each kind in the throwing hand while one is held. Each skull hangs from a knot of its own by a cord to the iron ring through its crown, swinging a little as the twin moves (`swing`), shown or left undrawn (`visible`: `lay`, from `userData.belt`, laid by the game each frame; undrawn, its triangles cost nothing, and the skulls showing make its pipelines): the belt shows what's left, and the one in hand is off the rope from its unhook to its release. The arm's throw is a reach to points through the action (`throwArm`: down to the rope's hook, back over the shoulder, out to the release at `a.hit`, back to where the pose has it), blended in and out over the action's first and last tenth. A belt skull is `beltSkull.ts`'s, one object on the rope, in the hand, in flight and lying on the floor (`twinSkulls.ts`): the crypt's skull with the twins' jaw shut on it (the house style of Reliquary's own small skulls), its glows small and deep in its sockets, drawn at `BELT_K` (2.4: at 1.9 a skull on the floor read as a white dot under its glow from the run's camera) on a rope of `ROPE_LONG` 1.45 and `ROPE_SAG` 0.6 so four of that size hang apart. Its first was meshed on a 4.5 cm grid to fit a boss's 45k triangles, four cells across a man's skull: from Kenneth, "Those skulls need work, improve model", a lumpy dome with two flat glows stuck on it. It's meshed fine now (5.5 mm) and simplified within 1 mm (about 1.6k triangles with its eyes and ring), and the triangles came from the twins' bones: every sculpted bone is simplified as it's meshed (`fewer` in `ossuary.ts`, three's meshoptimizer: a surface net lays as many triangles on a smooth shaft as round a socket, and the edge collapse keeps its vertices on the field's surface with their normals and shading), within an eighth of its grid's cell (`FEWER_ERROR`) and a twin's own head within a twenty-fifth (`HEAD_ERROR`: at an eighth Slab's crown showed facets and his crack faded). Slab 42.4k triangles, Reliquary 39.7k, the five skulls round Reliquary's casket back on their own grid (main's 42.2k and 43.9k; the coarser orbit of #900's first round is gone). The simplifier loads with the Twins' file (`readyOssuary`, the file's `ready`, which `loadFoe` waits for), so no bone is made before it. `lab twins --parts look` pictures each twin, its head and Reliquary's casket, and counts what each draws. An arm's `reach` is never quite straight (`REACH_MOST`, 0.97 of its length, and a way across it to its pole where the forearm lies along the upper arm): a throw's points past the arm's length made the axis across a straight arm none, its turn NaN.
