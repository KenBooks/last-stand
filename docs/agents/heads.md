# The heroes' heads

Part of [`src/entities/models/AGENTS.md`](../../src/entities/models/AGENTS.md), kept here as only work on a hero's head needs it: how `models/face.ts` and `models/head.ts` build the face, the hair, the beard and the neck, the fields a face is tuned by, and how a face is tuned against its reference with the face harness (`tools/facelab`). Read it before changing any of them, and add what you learn to it.

- `models/face.ts`: each hero's head as a signed distance field in millimetres, sculpted from average measurements, with a face shape (`FACES`) and colouring (`LOOKS`) per hero.
  - It's meshed by casting rays from inside the skull on a grid densest over the face (`headGrid`).
  - It's painted on the same grid in the loading workers (the `face` recipe in `core/pbr.ts`), so the texture fits a grid of any detail.
  - The rays start lower for the jaw and higher for the brow, so none skims along the skin. Past an overhang seen from the rays' start (under the brow, into the lid's crease) neighbouring rays meet the skin far apart; the points round such a stretched edge slide over the skin until they cover it (`relax`: the triangle between them would hang in the air as a dark streak), and the paint follows the moved points (`gridP`). A hollow inside the head still shows as streaks.
  - A face can be built on a measured mask (`FACES[...].mask`: both heroes', the mage's fitted to the real face nearest his old one, which had no picture: `dense.py`'s `nearest`) instead of blobs: its front's cross-section at each height a superellipse (`front` from the reference's profile, `half` from its front view, `side` where it's widest, `p` how square across, `pz` how its sides run back, `hollow` the soft hollow under the cheekbones), the nose, lips, brow, eyes and chin standing on it. Blobs joined by small blends left grooves, lumps and a snout; on a mask the cheekbones' blobs are left out too (they stood out of it with grooves round them). Don't tune the mask's knots by hand: `tools/facelab/fit.mjs` fits them (with the skull's, brow's, jaw's and chin's values) by least squares to the reference rebuilt in 3D and its front view's widths, then check the fit's clay views and curvature map.
- `models/head.ts` builds the head: skin, eyes under lids, ears, hair cards (alpha-tested strands), a full beard for a face with `beardFull` (a volume over the skin: the skin's grid points lifted along their rays by face.ts's `beardLift`, leaning down below the mouth so it hangs, painted with the face's own paint, its edges sunk into the skin; and short tufts of cards over its lower part and past its edge, not round the mouth. Cast as part of the skin's own distance field, its hanging underside came out as a row of teeth; as long cards hung from the jaw it was a ruff, and draped over the robe's cowl a sheet that read as a liquid; facelab's `beard.ts` measures it)), and the neck.
- Hair comes in styles (`buildHead`'s `hair`): `swept`, combed straight back over the head and lying on it down to the nape, ending there and behind the ears (the mage: hung straight down from where they left the head, its locks made a bob over the ears, and a thick cap of combed fur under few locks read as a wig; facelab's `hair.ts` measures a wig's edge, the locks' ends and the ears covered) or `loose`, thick and tousled to the chin (the warrior: parted a little off the middle, locks rooted along a part line running back from the hairline (never at one point: roots meeting at a point show as a starburst from above), swept out over the temples and falling in waves past the ears, the front lifted up and back off the forehead, a few locks hanging beside the forehead to frame it), each lock a great-circle path over the head kept clear of the skin and the ears by the head's distance field (`pushOut`), shaded on its own (vertex colours: darker at the root, in its waves' hollows, in the deeper layers and where it lies close to the head), its cards a dense clump texture (solid in the middle, darker at its edges: thin strands of high contrast read as wood grain at a distance) with a tapered root. Locks start flush with the scalp behind the hairline (a lock's square root end shows as a block), and the cap over the scalp rises gradually from behind it, across a hairline running steeply down at the temples too (a step within a grid cell shows as a stair); under loose hair the cap is plain and dark, the shadow between the locks. A third style, `mane` (the mage's), is `swept` grown long: more locks, no parting (the front and crown locks fall to a random side, the front rooted towards the middle), wavier, and each lock past where it leaves the head behind hung by its weight (`hang`: straight down to the collar, `HANG_END` in the head's mm, give or take `HANG_SPREAD`, its end flicking out). Its locks run down the head's surface only a little (`fall`): run far, their ends went down the neck and the hair reached the shoulders. Every style's cards are lit by the hair's mass, not each card's own face (`massNormals`: each normal turned 0.75 of the way to the direction out from the skull's centre, as sdf-harness lights hair): lit by its own face each card showed its black edge and the hair read as a helmet of black fringes.
- **Loose hair falls to the collar behind** (`BACK_END`, `BACK_SPREAD` in `head.ts`; the owner's "the hair cut off too early"): the locks down the back of the head ended between the mouth and the chin (-40 to 0 in the face's mm) while those at the sides fell 25 mm further, so from behind and from the low camera over the shoulder the hair was cut level above a tall bare neck. They end at -92 to -50 now, at the gambeson's collar. Same number of locks and cards (the geometry is still made once a face, hair and detail). Still thin at the very middle of the nape from straight behind, where a sliver of neck shows between the ends.
- **The warrior's lips sit in his face, not out of it** (`lipFwd` 16.5 to 10.5, `lips` 0.72 to 0.78, `mouthW` 1.07; the owner's "the lips should be more relaxed", from a profile): fitted to the reference's landmarks, the two rolls stood 5 mm out of the muzzle as a flat-fronted block with a hollow under the lower one, a pursed mouth from the side and from three-quarters. By eye, in `lab turnaround`'s head panels; the reference pictures weren't on the machine, so facelab's proportions weren't read again.
- Beyond the shape (`FACES`), a face is tuned by optional fields: `bridge`, `tip`, `browDrop`, `hollow`, `lowerW`, `masseter`, `beardLine`, `beardUnder`, `stacheCurve`, and the top of the neck under the jaw (`neckW`, `neckBack`, `neckBlend`: narrower, further back and blended less, the jaw stands out over it in a line, as a photo shows it; and the beard then grows on the jaw, not down the neck); a column neck (`neckCol`, the mage's: the head's skin ends in a column of the free neck's real section, an ellipse of a man's neck girth, ANSUR II's 0.69 of the head's, and the rig's neck keeps that section all the way up, crossing the head's skin by a millimetre each way at a seam that runs round it, `neckSeam`: low in front under a full beard's neckline, high behind so the neck bends above the collar; the neck's tube moves with the head near the seam through its own per-vertex skin blend, a `skinK` attribute `Sculpt.skin` reads, so the two skins stay matched as the head turns, is shaded with the normals of the tube without the crossing and has its grid's wrap seam welded, `weldNormals`; the face's pores and relief fade out down the column: a neck narrowing up to the jaw read as a cone, and the old seam showed as a step, a crease and a band of other colour; facelab's `neck.ts` measures the girth up the neck against the head's, the seam's step, crease, colour difference and relief, and skin showing through the collar). Every hero has one now. Its top (`COLUMN_TOP`) is drawn in at the sides under the ears and carried up behind into the skull's base: cut off level, it stood out of the narrower skin there as a ledge the light drew across the back of the neck (#261). Its paint fades into the tube's own colour by the seam. Do not paint the jaw's shadow on the neck, or shade the tube's vertices: the fixed patch read as a bulge under the ear in the mage's hood. Leave the neck to the scene's lighting and shadows, and fade the face's baked occlusion out where the neck column meets the head. Check the seam's colour in `lab neck` on all heroes when changing either mesh. The tube follows the head (`buildNeck` takes the head joint), and the rig turns both about joints in front of the column's middle, so the neck swings about 8 mm aside in its collar as he looks to a side: the collars are centred on it (`NECKLINE_BACK`, `necklineBack`) with room for that. `npm run lab -- neck` reads all of it on every hero, dressed and bare, at rest and looking aside; facelab's `neck.ts` predates column necks); in its look `hairDrop`, `templeDrop`, `nape` (the hairline lower behind the ears and down the nape, as short hair grows), `brows`, `browArch`, `browTail`, `browFill`, `browLift`, `pores`, `stubble` (a short-cropped beard), `beardClump` (its hairs as soft short strands in the head's own mm, the way they grow, in patches, thickest on the moustache and under the lower lip: hairs finer than a texel average out at a distance to one flat tone and the beard reads as a block, and a pattern thresholded hard reads as wood grain), `contour` (shading painted where light falls less), `lipTint`, `lipColor`, `noseWarm` (the nose's share of the flush), `browIn` (how far from the middle the brows start), `nostrils`, `under`, `ao` (occlusion baked into the paint from the distance field: the game's shadow map is about 2 cm a texel with a 3 cm bias, so a face gets no shadows of its own; with it the ears are shaded by vertex colours too and rougher, as vertex colours don't dim a highlight; the neck is left unshaded), `beardHair` (the beard's colour, the brows' near it), `eyeShade` (the upper lid's shadow painted on the eye, which never turns, and a dimmer white), and in the shape `lidSoft` (a lower lid blending into the cheek: a lid standing out of the socket casts a crescent), `lidFold` (a fleshy upper lid filling the socket up to the brow: a hooded, deep-set eye, not a pit), `browFwd`, `foreheadFwd`, `noseRoot`, `skullFwd`, `lipFwd`, `mouthUp` (the profile's depths and the mouth's height, fitted to the reference's side view; `mouthUp` warps the lower face's heights through `faceY`, and the paint follows it), `templeNarrow` (the skull narrower at the temples than behind the ears, so the face's sides run straight from the temples to the jaw) `eyeW` (the opening's half-width: past the eyeball's outline the opening shows the socket and the eye ends square), `gridEye` (more of the mesh's rows and columns over the eyes: at the grid's spacing an 8 mm opening fell between two rows and rendered as a slot full of iris) and `mask`. Each defaults to the old shape, so a hero not using one is unchanged. `Sculpt.add` / `skin` keep vertex colours only for a material that uses them.
- Tune a face against its reference with the face harness (`npm run facelab -- --ref <picture>`, `tools/facelab/README.md`): a studio render square to the face, lit as the reference is (`--key`), landmarks found the same way on both pictures and aligned by the eye corners, zoomed crops of each feature side by side and overlaid, and a report of proportions, the face's outline, the brows, colour and tone region by region (ours at the reference's resolution, warped onto its landmarks), the hair's outline, how it frames the forehead, its length, tones and the direction its strands run; the other views (side, back, from above) and which mesh shows where; not by screenshots judged by eye in a biome's light. Proportions can match while the look doesn't: when a face still doesn't look like its reference, add the measure that shows why to the harness before changing the face. The detector's points on our render follow its own idea of a face (they barely moved when our lips widened 13%, and drew an almond round an eye that rendered as a slot): measure ours from the render's own passes (the eyeball's mask) or its colours (the lips' width), not those points. Shape before colour: the clay views (no paint, no hair), a turnaround's three-quarter and side views (`--turn`: compared at the side panel's own turn, which needn't be a profile), landmarks placed by hand on the reference for what a beard and hair hide (`--points`: the jaw's angles and line, the face's sides), the relief (whether bones stand out, and whether that is the shape or the paint) and the surface everywhere (`--turn` also rebuilds the reference in 3D with a statistical model of real faces and maps ours against it, against the real face nearest ours, and our skin's curvature: outlines and points alone left the surface between them free, and it came out unlike a face); colour after.
- `toGroup` places anything by the head's anatomy (mm).
- `surface` gives the skin in any direction, and the helmet and hat are shaped with it.

- **Looks: a face and its hair a player picks** (`data/heroLooks.ts`, the owner's "customisation for all characters, mainly face hair hair colour... presets of 3-4 options, and let the user decide only hair colour"). Each class's first look is his face and hair as they were (`HERO_HEADS`, every player's until he picks another); the others are faces of their own in `face.ts`'s `VARIANTS`, the class's own face with some of its shape (`F`) and colouring (`L`) changed: the warrior's veteran is PR #551's scarred face (and its `scar` paint) with cropped hair, his northman and old soldier the earlier face options' D and A; the mage a wanderer (a short beard) and a sage (grey, a full beard, a mane); the ranger a tracker (stubble, cropped) and an elder (grey, a long beard). A face is a `FaceId`, keyed through the head's `once` cache, the workers' maps (`face` recipe) and grids as the heroes' own were. Only each class's look in the save slot is painted and made at the boot (`chooseLooks` in `game/looks.ts`, `preloadTextures`' faces, `chooseHead` and `preloadHeads`); another is painted by a maps worker of its own (`readyFace` in `core/textures.ts`) and its head made by a head worker (`readyHead`, named by its face, hair and crop: `specOfKey`) before a hero is built in it, as the hall does as it's picked (`restyleHallHero`) and a partner's copy (`buildableLook`: built in a look that's made meanwhile, and again once his has come). Painted on the page, a face held the main thread 1.5 s and more.
- **Hair colour is a uniform** (`Head.setHair`, `Player.setHair`): the hair's cards, its cap and a beard's tufts take the picked colour in their material colours (each its own multiple of it, as built from `L.hair`), and the painted face (brows, stubble, a full beard's volume, the scalp under the hair) is moved by `KitLooks.hair` in the face's own program (`kit.face`, `HAIR_SHIFT_GLSL` in `core/materials.ts`; the program key `entityfx-hair`, every face's whatever colour; WebGPU's in `kitNodes`). The paint is linear in its hair colours, so `paintFace` tracks, with each texel, how much of the scalp's hair colour is in it (`dh`: every hair colour mixed taken as a share of `L.hair` by luminance, `hairK`, at most `HAIR_K_MOST`, 1.4: the warrior's beard, 2.7 times as bright as his hair, took a blond pick past white; every later mix, shade and occlusion scaling it as it scales the colour) and keeps it in the roughness map's blue (`HAIR_SHARE_MOST` at full; the roughness is green, as three reads it). The shader adds that share times the picked colour less the face's own, in the paint's sRGB. Grey hairs (`grey`) stay grey. One vec3 uniform, in the face's program alone (no arrays: the phone's 40 vectors of room, `src/core/AGENTS.md`). `npm run lab -- herolooks` measures every look and the colours.
