# Views and the aim

From `src/core/AGENTS.md`: the camera's views (V) and how the cursor and the eye's ray pick what an attack goes at. Paths are under `src/core/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **Views** (V, in runs and the lobby; on touch the camera button right of the pause button, a finger dragged over the world looking round in the close views, `lookByDrag` in `ui/touch.ts`, no pointer locked): `core/renderer.ts` has top-down, third and first person, gliding between them. The canvas is the window's own on every device (`frameRect`, `lab resize`): the screen-sized canvas touch devices had, drawn into at its top left to spare a drawing buffer's reallocation on going full screen (#787), was the WebGL renderer's and went with it. The close views lock the pointer for mouse look through the run (the bank-or-continue choice too: B / C pick), and in the lobby while its panels are stowed (V into a close view puts them away; opened again, by I or at the chest, they have the pointer free for them; its view shift beside a panel is the top-down view's alone) (`wantPointerLock` in `core/input.ts`, taken while the last key press or click still counts as a user gesture, else on a click, never with Esc down or just let go: from a panel Esc closed, the browser let the lock go again for that Esc and the pause menu opened, `lab village --only escClosesHelp`) and aim at the crosshair (`input.centerAim`): the foe under it, else the floor, else a point `aimRange` ahead. Losing the lock mid-wave pauses. **A lock is lost only when the game didn't let go of it itself** (`letGo` in `input.ts`, #554): the browser answers the game's own letting go (the pause menu opening) from its own process, a frame or more late, and judged by whether the lock was wanted when the answer came, an Esc that closed the menu meanwhile had it open again. Nor is the lock asked for again before its loss is heard (`heldAsHeard`): a browser clears `pointerLockElement` as it lets the lock go and tells the page in an event after it, and a frame between the two took the lock back, the Esc never pausing. The Esc that lost the lock is dropped only until it's let go (`escapeSpentOnLock`: a browser handing that keydown over too sends it before the keyup), never a second press, which was dropped for 300 ms and left the menu open; a keyboard's repeats are no presses (Chrome keeps the Esc that let the lock go and hands the page its repeats, which read as a press, the first keydown never having come), and full screen left by the same Esc as the lock is no second Esc however late its change comes (`lockLostWithin` in `ui/autoFullScreen.ts`'s `keepEsc`). `npm run lab -- cursor --parts esc` presses Esc again and again as each browser hands it over (`--canary` reads it as before). A move under the lock far larger than the one before it is the browser's own and turns nothing (`lookMove` in `core/input.ts`: Chrome on Windows sends one now and then, about half the window across, as it puts its hidden cursor back, and a long spin jerked the view round by about 180 degrees, #318; `npm run lab -- spin`). Held Alt keeps the lock and stills the view, the mouse moving a cursor drawn for the HUD instead (`ui/altCursor.ts`, which hears the mouse before `input.ts` does). In first person the body faces where the camera looks (`lookFacing`), so melee arcs and blocking follow the view, and only its arms, hands and weapons show (`entities/viewModel.ts`): the posed model (at its own scale) is moved so the camera sits just above its neck (the head rides the spine, so a lean never brings the shoulders under the camera), turned and tilted with the view and tipped back a little so the hands sit in frame, and the rest (pauldrons included) goes on a layer the camera doesn't draw. Every attack animates as in the other views, and **the middle of the view stays clear**: weapons, hands and spell effects are seen but kept off it, so what's ahead (the foes) is. `Model.firstPerson` switches a model's first-person poses, tuned by measuring where the weapon and hands land on screen (`FP_*` in `models/warrior.ts` and `models/mage.ts`): the warrior holds its weapons low in the corners, damps the stride's arm swing, swings lower and narrower with the blade up so the swing crosses the view broadside, places Power Strike, Twin Fangs and Bull Rush in the view itself (`holdInView` with `fromEyes` in `viewModel.ts`: a point of the camera's view in the posed model's world, each arm reaching by IK and the wrist turning the weapon onto a direction; `FP_CHOP`, `FP_CUT`, `FP_TUSK`): Power Strike crosses the blades over the top of the view as the charge builds and drives them down through it, Twin Fangs cocks each blade across the upper view and cuts it down to the opposite corner, Bull Rush draws the blades back, drives them ahead through the rush and flings them out to both sides as it lands (in every view: the charge's pose follows through for a moment after the rush, its weapons sweeping out with slash trails, and through the eyes the view widens with the speed, `kickFov`, as streaks of air rush at it); it raises its shield to just under the crosshair and hides its elbow fur; a two-hander (whose lower grip, the left hand's, would sit below the frame otherwise) is always placed in the view: at rest in both hands low on the right, the right elbow kept down so the forearm rises from the frame's edge, its swing sweeping the blade across the view like a wiper, Power Strike a raised guard on the right and the blow ahead; the mage holds the staff out to the right, narrows its bell sleeves and plays the free hand's gestures (cast, Void Lance) in the left of the view, shifting the hand in the chest's space and reaching it with arm IK (`reachArm`). The third-person camera pulls in towards the character only in the village, in front of what's built behind him (`boomClear`, against `G.arena.wall`: dissolved, a palisade behind him left a small window of the frame and the rest black, "being behind obstacle - this is not useful... and a last stop on where it ends, `cameraClear`, as the boom's least put it inside a wall pressed against, whose far side showed through), and short of any elevated floor (`floorReach` / `cameraFloorAt` reading `groundHeight` round the camera with `CAMERA_FLOOR_CLEAR` = 0.4 m, `nearClear` checking near-plane corners against floor too, and the final clamp reading both hero floor and `cameraFloorAt(cam.x, cam.z)` so pitching up on the terrace stairs or before the hall's dais pulls the boom in over the edge rather than sinking under the portico floor or scraping behind its lip; `lab village --only cameraOverFloors`, its canary `holdCameraFloorHeroOnly`), and under 1.4 m it comes in over his head with a near plane of 0.12 m (`overHead`, `BOOM_NEAR`, its view wider, `BOOM_WIDER`: at the shoulder's side what it left was a slice of his back, cut by the near plane, "you need to make sure it looks good at all angles"), the props' hole shut as it does (nothing stands between, and the cone down to him opened the wall beside the camera onto the outside; not through the eyes), so give a biome's prop and wall materials `seeThrough(m, 'prop')` (not the floor's; low foliage too: the canopies' wide hole took the ferns round him wherever he stood), and a raw `ShaderMaterial` on a prop (the gates' membranes, the beacons' flames) `seeThroughFade`, its fragment shader fading by `seeFade()`: the chunk patch can't reach it, and the membrane hid the hero standing in a gate. One whose fragments are a proxy's, not what it draws (the flames' box, ray-marched), fades as one by `seeFadeAt` at points along the view through its middle, averaged: the hole ends in a plane across the view, which cut the fire in two on the box's faces as the camera came near (`lab fire` reads it), and by its middle alone the whole fire would fade at once as that plane passed it.
- **An attack goes the way the body faces.** The cursor aims at the floor (or a prop) as it is seen (`floorHit` in `core/input.ts`: the dais's top, its step, then the floor; met on the floor's plane, the cursor over the dais pointed 35 cm past what it showed and the hero turned away from it near the feet), the body turns from its own position to that point, and a shot leaving a weapon's tip heads the way the body aims, closing on the point only from a few metres out (`Player.shotHeading`: aimed from the staff's tip, out beside the body, a point near the feet sent the bolt sideways or back). Measure with the aim probe: the cursor at screen offsets round the feet, on the dais and off it, zoomed in and out, the shot's heading and the body's facing against the point under the cursor.
- **The aim meets a body as it's drawn** (`aimAlong` in `core/input.ts`): the first thing the eye's ray meets, of a foe its rigid meshes and its skinned limbs alike (`castSolid` in `fx/stuckArrows.ts`, the skinned through `entities/models/hulls.ts`), looked for as far as its body reaches as built (`Enemy.reach`: a boss's crown stood 1.8 times the height its hits count). Met on the rigid meshes alone within its height, the aim over a boss's limbs and crown went on to the floor behind it, and Quarry Mark marked a small foe there. The foe met is kept (`input.foe`, none for touch): a skill that picks a foe takes it, not the foe nearest the point. Measure with `npm run lab -- held --class ranger` (every point over each biome's boss and its summons, in each view) and its cost with `--profile=aimAlong`. **The crosshair's point on a body is no nearer the hero than 5 m** (`AIM.near`, across the floor): nearer, it goes on along the crosshair's ray to there (the floor or the aim's range at the most), the foe met still the one aimed at. The hero aims from his own place at the point and the third-person camera stands behind and beside him, so a point on a foe hugging him is beside him: his aim wheeled 75 degrees to it and back as its paw crossed the crosshair, Void Lance's whole beam with it (#482, 4 flashes a second). From 5 m the two lines are 6 degrees apart, and what's under the crosshair nearer is still in the aim's way. The mouse's aim in the top-down view is as it was. `npm run lab -- nearaim` holds it. A point the crosshair meets on a foe's head is taken `AIM_INTO` in along its ray, not towards the body's middle (#750: a headshot aimed at a small head was taken down to its neck); `npm run lab -- headshots`.
