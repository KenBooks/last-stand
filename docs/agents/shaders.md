# Compiling programs

From `src/core/AGENTS.md`: what a program costs, where it's compiled and how the warm-ups keep compiles out of play (the root's "Shaders compile at load, never mid-game" is the rule). Paths are under `src/core/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **A lit program has 40 uniform vectors of room on a phone** (`npm run lab -- programs --limits`): the largest read 184 fragment uniform vectors (20 point lights, their packed bounds, the shadows, the kit's own) against the 224 any WebGL 2 device must give, and an S25 Edge's 256. A desktop gives 1024, so a material that spends more compiles and draws on every PC here and draws nothing on a phone, saying nothing: the heroes' palettes' six tables of 16 rows took them to 272 and the phone's hero was his hair and his shadow (#470, reverted). Tables of values go in a small data texture read by `texelFetch`, not in uniform arrays. Run `--limits` for any change to a material or shader, and read the phone's own report after trying it on a preview: it names the programs its driver refused (`auditPrograms` in `shaders.ts`).
- **Compiling is a first visit's cost, a lit program's most** (`npm run lab -- programs --cost`: each of the boot's programs compiled again alone, salted so no cache answers, its canary the cache): ANGLE's D3D compiler took about 0.56 s over a lit program written out for 16 point lights, 0.05 s over an unlit one. three writes the point lights' loop out once a light (`#pragma unroll_loop_start`, for a light's shadow map); so only the two point lights that cast (each biome's warmest, `staticShadow` in `world/props.ts`; three puts them first) are written out and the rest are lit in a loop (`loopPointLights` in `renderer.ts`: with one casting, three wrote every point light out again): the programs' 40 s of compiling fell to 22 to 27 s, the pictures and the frame's GPU time the same. Keep a material's variants few (a uniform, not a define: the kit's near glow and rim in `materials.ts`, 157 programs to 146), and see what one costs before adding it. A page can't keep a program itself (WebGL has no program binaries): the GPU's process keeps them by their source, so a return reads them back in a few ms, and a deploy that leaves a shader's source as it was keeps its cache. A warm-up asks whether a program is linked only after a fence placed behind its compile has passed (`commandsDone` in `shaders.ts`): each question is a round trip to the GPU's process, and asked while it was reading programs back it held the lobby up 250 ms at a time.
- **Behind the lobby, a warm-up is a step a frame and draws only its part** (`warmUp`'s `step`, `drawn`, `giveUpMs`): each root's compile in slices of about 8 ms, a few meshes at a time (`COMPILE_SLICE_BEHIND_MS`, `onlyThese`: the rest's materials set aside for the call, as three's compile passes over a mesh with none; a part's root compiled in one task held an S25 Edge's village 160 to 215 ms a part, its perf traces, and its longest step is the warm-up's `compileStepMs`, which `stalls` lists), a 2 ms slice of uploads, each program's uniforms read and the draw in a frame of its own (each cached program is read back on the GPU process's main thread, which draws every frame of the browser, and a whole part's in one frame dropped several), its part alone on the warm-ups' layer into a pixel of the target (the scene drawn again for each part held the lobby up), and what it adds kept out of the lobby's frames till its programs are linked: three links a program as it first draws it and waits there, so a foe seated in its crowd (which draws it whatever the camera sees) or an object unculled before its link had the lobby's frame wait for it (a return's lobby drew 18 to 36 frames a second). A program not linked 150 ms after its commands are done is cold: the part is given up, nothing drawn (`cold`). What a draw itself makes (a shadow's depth programs, the outline's masks) still links inside it. `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot` counts the lobby's frames a second as the perf overlay does (`--cold-return` for a return whose fight was never compiled).
- **Shaders ANGLE compiles at a draw** (D3D11; `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --angle`: `D3DCompile` on the GPU process's main thread): a vertex attribute of integers read as floats is a format D3D11 converts, and a `flat` varying under GL's last-vertex convention needs a geometry shader, and for each ANGLE compiled a shader of its own at the program's first draw, on the main thread, holding every frame up (a first visit's 760 ms). So the bones' indices are floats (`models/shapes.ts`, `handShape.ts`) and a `flat` varying is the first vertex's (`firstVertexProvokes` in `renderer.ts`, WEBGL_provoking_vertex: the crowds' rows, the same at a triangle's every vertex). A return reads such shaders back with the program; keep integer data out of float attributes.
- **Shadows' programs don't hang on the casters' order** (`steadyShadowPrograms` in `shaders.ts`, at the renderer's set-up): three draws every caster that tests no alpha with one shared depth material, copies the caster's `map` onto it, and builds it a new program only when the instancing, instance colours or skinning change between draws, so which variant (with a map, or without) a caster got hung on what was drawn before it, and a new order mid-fight compiled one more. The map, which does nothing without an alpha test, is let go before each such draw. A program compiled mid-fight shows in the perf report by its kind (`depth`: a shadow); `npm run lab -- compiles` breaks every kind of prop and fights a first wave in each biome (`--boot low@518400` as a phone boots) and names the draw that first used it.
- **Shadows' programs are compiled with the rest** (`compileShadows` in `shaders.ts`, from `compileAhead` and `warmUp`): three's compile makes none (its shadow pass makes each as it first draws with it, linked there, the page held up: a first visit's lobby warm-up made 10 inside its draw), so each caster is compiled with a stand-in for the material the pass would draw it with (r185's `getDepthMaterial`: its `customDepthMaterial`, else three's own; for a point light's pass, which a still shadow's is, its `customDistanceMaterial`, else three's distance material, its side, alpha test and map as the pass sets them, the map let go as `steadyShadowPrograms` lets it go), without the scene's fog and environment (the pass draws with neither), and the warm-up waits for their links. The pass's own materials then find them made. What the shadow map alone draws (`inShadowsOnly` in `world/culling.ts`) is left out of the colour compile (`withoutUndrawn`, with the crowds' bodies' own meshes). `npm run lab -- programs` lists each program a draw made: the boot's are 0 (#275's: 32).
  - **The resolution is counted in the screen's pixels, never by the OS's scaling** (as a 4K video plays at 4K at any scaling): every level renders all of them, on any device: what a level cuts is other work, never the resolution (from play, "I want to run the game at full phone res, if we need to cut, we need to cut something else, not resolution"; Medium rendered 2560x1440's worth and Low 1920x1080's, and Auto halved the pixels down to 960x540's). A device that can't hold its target on Low at its screen's pixels stays slow there: the 4K laptop of trace BN6P-FN12 drew its scene in 4.6 ms at 0.52 Mpx, about 73 ms at its own 8.3 Mpx, and the owner chose that over a cut resolution. What the hardware holds is measured, not guessed from what it is (a phone, a desktop, a card's name). The player may choose fewer themself: the pause menu's Resolution (`setResolution` in `quality.ts`, the cookie `last-stand-resolution`, `setResolutionShare` in `renderer.ts`: 100%, 75%, 50% or 25% of the screen's pixels each way, folded into `sceneShare` with Auto's budget), which no level and never Auto touches (from play, for a device still lagging on Very low: "resolution reducing ... 100%, 75, 50 and so on"). `pixels` stays for a harness's budget (`useQuality`, the lab's `--quality low@518400`). In px per css px, a 4K screen at 300% (1280x720 css px) rendered 1920x1080 stretched to 4K, where at 100% the same screen rendered 4K, on an RTX 4080 that drew the frame in 3.3 ms. A share of the screen's pixels is rounded to a power of two's fraction (`SHARE_STEP`), so the same screen at any scaling renders the same.
  - **Auto starts at Low on every device** (`autoStart` in `quality.ts`, where it has no saved level: a first visit or Auto chosen again). High's boot work happens before Auto can measure the device: integrated-GPU report N8DN-CDRW took 27.6 s to reveal the lobby, then stepped through Medium and Low to Very low. Low avoids High's HDR, MSAA and animated loading exit at boot. Explicit presets and previously settled Auto levels remain respected; Auto still tries a level above a settled one on later boots. `lab auto` checks the new default separately from downgrade cases seeded at High.
  - **Auto steps the levels down, the pixels kept**: with the frame rate under `minFps` over a 4 s window, a level down; far below its target, to Low at once: once a second whose middle frame is under half `minFps` (a hitch doesn't move the middle; from play, a laptop's integrated GPU switched to Auto, which starts over from High, ran at 5 frames a second "for the first minute or two"; `npm run lab -- auto --screen 1432x768@2`: to Low in 3.4 s, 12.5 at the old pace, and a fast device with an 800 ms hitch every 2 s never stepped). Each step is marked in the performance trace (`quality auto: low`). What it settles on is kept as its level (`last-stand-quality-auto`; an older Auto's budget of pixels after an `@` is let go). It never steps back up within a session; a boot starts at the settled level (the warm-ups and the loading screen's exit too: Low's plain fade on a phone) and, once its frames are the device's, tries the level above, kept and saved once a 4 s window holds `minFps`, else left at once for the settled level alone, the cookie as it was (`bootAuto`; each marked in the trace, `quality auto: trying high`, `high kept`): a step down was kept for good, and a 4070 Ti slowed once by other work on its GPU sat on Medium from then on (trace 0HSJ-60QV). The trial is left at its first quick window under `minFps` itself, not half of it: judged by the 4 s path, a phone settled on Medium ran High at 26 to 47 frames a second for 6 s every boot, a 0.5 s stall as it switched, and sent a performance report for it (trace 9JA7-BSSS). A miss puts the next trial off for a boot, and each miss after for twice as many, up to 8 (`last-stand-quality-trial`, `skip/gap`; a level kept or Auto chosen again clears it), so a device that can't hold the level above pays its 3 s once in a while, not every boot. The trial's frames are Auto's, not the device's pace at its level: the performance recorder marks them settling, out of play (`autoTrying`), its stalls still counted. The lab's `--boot` sets the level as the player's setting for that reason (Auto's trial would keep the level above on the lab's steady frames). A frame that isn't the device's measure is left out all the same: Auto holds (`holdQuality`) while a warm-up runs behind the lobby or under the fight's loading bar (`game/warm.ts` `isWarming`; compiled cold behind the lobby, a return drew 0 to 6 frames a second for 3 s, enough to step a level down for good). The probe of it fed Auto slow frames and read each step's canvas, scene and cookie (`--screen` at 1x, 2x and 3x; one 4K screen at 100% and 300% gave the same lines).
