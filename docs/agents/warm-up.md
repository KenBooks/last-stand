# The warm-up

From `src/game/AGENTS.md`: what the boot makes and hands to the engine before the lobby shows (`warmup.ts`), and what's warm and what waits for it (`warm.ts`). Paths are under `src/game/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `warmup.ts`: one of everything that brings its own materials made and handed to the engine, whose pipelines for it are waited for, so nothing compiles mid-fight (the root's pipeline rule; `warmUp` in `core/shaders.ts` compiles and draws nothing of three's now). What follows of programs compiled, linked, given up as cold or stepped behind the lobby is how three's renderers were warmed, kept for what was measured: the parts, their order, what's built ahead and what waits for which need are live. The lobby's before it shows: its village compiled as it's built (`compileTheBiome`, a light standing in for the hero's), its effects, loot and the shown class's skills compiled with the hero as he's built (`compileTheLobby`: compiled with the lobby's warm-up, after the lobby's panels were painted, their links began 0.6 s after they were asked for), and all drawn by `warmLobby` with the hero wearing everything he can, the other biomes left out; another class's skills are warmed with its hero (`heroParts`; `npm run lab -- compiles --switch <class>`); at boot only the lobby's own hero is, and on a first visit (which opens on the hero selection screen) the hall's room with it (`warmLobby(true)`), the other heroes right after the loading screen leaves, a hero at a time, each shown on his plinth once he's warm, his tab's bar and the choice saying how far along he is till then and the choice waiting (`heroShare`, `heroWarming` in `warm.ts`; from play, "we can only load currently selected/default hero... and then load the two others, show some loading bars on those heroes": warmed with the lobby's, a low-end laptop's loading screen held; `npm run lab -- ui --states heroesWarming`); the rest after it (`warmTheRest`), as parts (a foe kind, another biome drawn in place of the lobby's, another class's hero with everything it can wear), built where the lobby's camera never looks and never let act. The fight's foes are built before the lobby shows, as its warm-up waits on the GPU (`buildAhead`: a kind's first build, its geometry sculpted and its crowd's merged, is one task of 30 to 90 ms, and built behind the lobby each held it up as long; a return's reveal came 0.2 to 0.4 s later), and kept out of the scene and their crowd till warmed. `warm.ts`: what's warm, and what waits for it (`whenWarm`): the fight's parts are warmed behind the lobby once the loading screen has left (its logo's exit draws over the lobby till then: `loadingLeft`), one at a time, a step a frame (`core/shaders.ts` `warmUp`'s `step`: a return's lobby kept 126 to 135 frames a second at the least, where with a part's steps back to back it drew 18 to 36), where they come from the GPU's cache; else once Start or a biome pick needs them, under the fight's loading bar (`ui/readying.ts`: about 1 s on a first visit, where the lobby's frames had linked the foes' programs one by one, waiting for each, for 5 s), four parts at a time (`PARTS_AT_ONCE`), on WebGL each lot's programs linked before the next lot's were compiled, the link wait letting the page go every 40 ms (`warmInTasks`: on the owner's PC a first visit's cold Start took 7.3 s where all at once it took 4.4 and a part at a time 17.4, its longest frame 90 ms where at once it was 1.6 s; compiled all in one go, a laptop's integrated GPU had them all to link at once, the page's own frames waiting behind them, and without KHR_parallel_shader_compile every program was linked in one task; from play, a low-end laptop joining a room froze its tab, "Chrome offers to kill the tab, I wait, and it offers second time"; `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --variants base,noParallelCompile`); co-op waits for the heroes alone, not the fight (a guest's start readies it under the fight's bar, as the host's Start does); the other heroes' (and the hall of heroes' room) behind the fight's where they come from the GPU's cache, else when Change hero, a save slot or co-op pick needs them, or the hero nears the village's hall where they stand, a hero at a time (on the first visit after a deploy, asked for as he neared the hall, they were compiled at once and held the village up 0.5 to 0.9 s, from play "lag when entering hall of heroes"; warmed under the loading screen then, they held a low-end laptop's, so they're not any more) (a hero's first build held the lobby up 250 ms: his head's hair). Under their bar a hero's head is made in a task of its own before his body (`Part.ahead`, `makeHeadAhead` in `models/head.ts`, the heads by model in `HERO_HEADS`), and the hall's heroes are built after them, a task each (`buildHallAhead` in `hall.ts`), so the hall opens on heroes kept: on a return the owner's PC held the bar 618 ms in one task (trace FD2V-NB48), each hero built whole (head and body, 210 to 350 ms) and then the three hall heroes in the opening's task (290 to 400 ms); now 200 ms at most, the ranger's head alone (`npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --change-hero`). A head split further needs the locks and the beard apart: they draw from one random stream in that order, and a head made a piece at a time would grow other beards. Warm is a build that warmed the fight on this machine before (`last-stand.fight-warmed` in local storage: the builds by their code's chunk, so a deploy that changed a shader is new) with its maps kept: kept maps alone said nothing of the programs, and a return after a visit that never fought compiled the fight cold behind the lobby, 0 to 6 frames a second for 3 s (`npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --cold-return`). A part with a program not linked 150 ms after its commands were done is cold too, given up before its draw. A first visit's other heroes are built under the loading screen after the fight's foes (`buildHeroesAhead`: built by their part, each was a task of 78 to 90 ms with the hall on the screen), and their warm-up then goes a step a frame wherever no loading bar covers the page (`warmInTasks`' `stepBehind`, `patient`: never given up on as cold, as a first visit's programs are; in one go a lot's compile was 30 ms, and a hero chosen in it held the hall up 104 ms), and under a loading bar in slices of 12 ms, the page let go to its next frame between them (`inSlices`: each lot's compile in one task held the fight's bar up 30 to 64 ms; let go at every step, a return's bar stayed up 1.4 to 1.6 s where main's took 0.3 to 0.6; let go by a message, or by a timeout, Chrome ran the slices back to back, the bar's frames waiting behind them: a first visit's held 139 to 357 ms, trace GRBF-8PBJ), its waits for links asking a frame apart (`WarmUp.poll`: given the slice as their wait, they spun `isReady` through it, 89 ms of a 139 ms frame), no more than 12 of its programs handed to ANGLE before they link (`LINKING_MOST`, `WarmUp.linkingMost`: a first visit's fight handed it 4 parts' at once, every core's thread compiling them, and the GPU's process's main thread, which links Chrome's own programs and presents every frame, waited among them, a frame held 347 ms), and the parts' builds a frame apart (a hero's head and body, up to 200 ms each, ran back to back); `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot` lists every frame of the lobby held up 50 ms or more. Each part is timed as a user timing measure, `warm <part>` (the stalls' boot lists the longest steps). The load is marked as the lobby is lit (`markLoaded` in `main.ts`): a program a warm-up makes after it is the boot's (`inTurn`), any other counts in the perf report as compiled late (marked once the fight's were warm, a new build's lobby counted every program late: `npm run lab -- programs --cold`).
- **The fight warms under the loading screen** (`warmTheRest`, `FIGHT_UNDER_LOADING` in `main.ts`; decided on three's WebGPU renderer): behind the lobby, its node builds and pipelines held the owner's lobby at 66 to 108 frames a second for its first 8 s, where WebGL's starts at the screen's rate. It costs the loading screen 3.4 s on a return (revealed at 13.6 s against 10.2) and 8 s on a first visit (20.5 against 12.6), and Start no longer waits on a bar.
- **A return's boot asks for its pipelines as soon as what draws with them is built, and waits for none of them there** (`sceneAhead`, `fightAhead` in `warmup.ts`; #843): a warm-up hands the engine its own samples and then waits for their pipelines (`warmUp` in `src/core/shaders.ts`), so each wait began only when the one before it was over, with the page idle through each. A laptop's boot waited 3.3 s at the lobby's warm-up, then 3.2 s at the fight's, then 2.6 s in its last step (trace 1FJ3-5Z5H): the first scene pipelines were asked for 13 s in, the fight's only once the lobby's were all made, and 16 of those the lobby's first frame draws with only as that frame was drawn, since nothing handed the village over. Now the village is handed over at "Kindling the lamps", the hero and the effects with him, and from then on the other heroes' skills, the foes' shots and each foe as it's built, beside the boot's next steps (the skills and the shots first: quick to make, and most of the fight's pipelines); the warm-ups after find them asked for. Only what's shown is handed over (the houses' rooms and the hall stay the engine's own walk's, behind the lobby), each mesh once. `npm run lab -- pipelines` lists the pipelines asked for step by step and fails on one the lobby draws with first asked for in the last step (16 on main before, 0 now); the fight's 16 moved from "Readying the fight" to "Summoning the hero", 78 asked in all before and after. By the work done (`npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --url ref:origin/main,this`'s `worked till the reveal`; the clock on this shared PC is no measure), a return, one boot a side: the village handed over, the page's main thread 4.44 s on a CPU before and 4.43 s after, the GPU's process 24.1 s and 24.4 s, "Lighting the lobby" 1.04 s to 0.08 s; then the fight handed over, the page 6.16 s and 5.61 s, the GPU's process 25.3 s and 22.7 s, "Readying the fight" 0.92 s to 0.11 s, the fight warm at 6.69 s to 5.48 s and the reveal 9.70 s to 8.17 s. Two boots of the same code differ by about 8% in that work (a first visit's, unchanged: 4.27 s and 4.62 s, 35.3 s and 38.0 s), so the sums say no work was added, not that some was saved. **Not a first visit's** (`sceneAheadOn`): it opens on the hall of heroes with the village hidden, and has the hall to ready under its loading screen; the village handed over there read 6 s more of the GPU's process's work in one boot (31.4 s to 37.8 s, less sure than it looked: see the 8%). Not measured on the laptop that reported it, nor on a phone. Left: the other heroes' pipelines (17 on this PC) are asked for as each hero is built behind the lobby, and compile as it's revealed.
