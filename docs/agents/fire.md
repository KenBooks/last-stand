# Fires on props

From `src/world/AGENTS.md`: the fluid fire on the crypt's and the Thornwood's props and the village's campfire (`fire.ts`, `fireBake.ts`, `fireNodes.ts`), measured with `npm run lab -- fire` and `npm run fire-bake`. Paths are under `src/world/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **Fires on props** (`buildFire` in `world/fire.ts`): flames, a bed of coals, embers and smoke, and the flicker of the biome's light over them. The flames are a fluid, simulated once (`fireBake.ts`, its worker `fireBake.worker.ts`, kept in `core/kept.ts`) after Fire Pro's physics (https://github.com/dgreenheck/threejs-fire-pro, MIT: buoyancy, vorticity confinement, a pressure projection, MacCormack transport, a flame's life burning down; it runs them on the GPU each frame by WebGPU alone): a 60 by 108 by 60 grid of a flame's life, on a velocity grid three times coarser, 3.8 s of a worker on a first visit, its 2 s loop of 60 frames played by every fire at one moment, each its own way round and puffing on its own swell, on WebGL, WebGPU and meshlets alike (from play, "we need better fire effects, more professional", "this is real fire", "well try to implement as close as you can"). What's kept is the box of the grid the flames ever reach (`KEPT`, two empty cells round it: 3% of the grid is ever flame), each frame as runs of empty cells and the lives between them (`encodeFrame`; a life the ray-march never draws, a byte of 1 or 0, stored as none), 987 KB in memory and across visits; on the GPU a `Data3DTexture` of two slots, the two frames drawn now (frame f in slot f mod 2), 416 KB, one frame decoded into it as the loop comes to it (`showMoment`, 0.1 ms, 30 times a second). The whole loop on the GPU was 23 MB, the game's download 3 MB, and passed two reviews (from the owner, "wth dude, our whole game is 5mb or something"); `npm run fire-bake` says each size and it and `lab:test` (`test/fireBake.test.mjs`) fail over its budget (`BUDGET_KB`: 1200 kept, 640 on the GPU) or with flame cut off outside the box. Every fire at one moment is what holds the GPU to two frames: the hall's six fires, each at a moment of its own, would want twelve. Until the worker comes the volume is 1 by 1 by 1 and empty, so no program changes, and a first visit's fires burn no flames for those seconds; the lab's pages wait for it (`flamesCome`, from `mapsCome`). Each pixel's ray marches a box over the bed (`STEPS`, started at random a pixel: in step the slices showed), gathering each flame's light by its life (`FLAME_LOOK.blackbody`), and only the hot gas holds back what's behind it (`holdsFrom`: held back as much as it gave, a flame's edge was a dark brown smear by day and its body an opaque tan; premultiplied, as light added alone washed out over daylight). Only the box's faces are depth-tested, so the march stops itself at what's solid inside it, as a depth test would: a brazier's bowl (`bowl`, `uBowl`: from below its flames showed through it, #291) and a bonfire's logs (`logs`, `uLogs`, flat-ended cylinders as thin as their tips (capsules cut a dome round each tip), `LOGS_MOST`: the logs inside the box were drawn over by the flames behind them and those reaching out of it hid the flames in front, "half of campfire ignores it, while the other half does not"; `lab fire` reads both). Sheets turned to the camera turned with it as it went round them ("fire rotates when camera rotates"): the box stands unturned over the bed. What a review kept failing: an even bed drawn in evenly stands as one smooth tent, so the bed burns in patches and its air is stirred (`stir`, which past about 5 lays it against the grid's wall); the grid is too coarse for fine strands close to, so the loop is read again finer through each flame (`strands`). Try a value in seconds with `npm run fire-bake -- key=value` (its picture isn't the game's light), then `lab fire`, `lab village --only campfireBurns`. WGSL's port is `fireNodes.ts`, line for line. The flames' box stays out of the meshlets (`noMeshlets`: a batch's copy drew them as a pale mound). A bonfire's light is soft close to (`BONFIRE_LIGHT`, a decay of `2 + LAMP_SOFT`: at 18 cd, under the village's lamps it added 14 levels of red, "there is no light from fire", `campfireBurns` holds it to 30; by the square alone at 85 cd a hero beside it burned white, "blinding reflections from fire", `heroBesideFire` reads his figure there, 14% burned out before). On WebGPU `softPointLights` in `core/lampNodes.ts` makes three's falloff the same, as it takes a decay as an exponent. A fire a metre across puffs about 1.5 times a second, and one swell moves its flames' height and its light together. Close up the flames cost 0.15 to 0.25 ms of a frame on a desktop GPU (`lab fire --cost`). The bed and a log's charred end are `emberMat`: a lit standard material, black char and grey ash glowing through Worley cracks (most of them dark: lit throughout, they read as lava), a bed's lumps over their sides too but not their tops, its sheen cut to a fifth (at a standard material's 4%, the char a metre under the light read as salmon clay). A fire's `at` is in the group it's built in, its shader, embers and smoke told its place in the world: a group moved and turned threw theirs from the group's own numbers, onto the plaza (the yard's gate torches, gone since, "weird particles like fire burning", `lab village --only firesWhereTheyStand`). A fire takes the biome's own point light, one of its ten: never add one for it. Its flames' light may be less than a fire's own (`FireSpec.glow`, the `uGlow` uniform): from the hall of heroes' low, near camera a ray through a brazier's foot crosses the most flame, and at the crypt's it burned white there, which the owner took for a glaring reflection on the bowl (hiding the bowl and the coals in turn left it, hiding the flames took it away); its braziers burn at half. Its flames fade with the prop under it (`seeThroughFade`), and its updater stops when the prop breaks (`groupLook`). A bonfire's logs are one bark mesh and one char mesh (`leanLog` in `forest.ts`). The forest clears the ground round its bonfires (`clearOfFires`): no fallen leaf within the ring of stones, and a fern's roots clear of the flames by its fronds' reach (0.9 m a unit of its scale: grown beside a bonfire, ferns spread over the flames), a spot inside moved out past the clearing rather than drawn again (another draw moves every prop placed after it). `npm run lab -- fire` measures each biome's fire against main's (`tools/lab/COMMANDS.md`): its look, whether it turns with the camera, the plants over it; the candles still burn the old way. The campfire's light is soft close to (`BONFIRE_SOFT`) and by day is `CAMPFIRE_BY_DAY` of its night's (a hand beside it, the sun on it too, burned white: from play, "fire blinding skin issue again"; `lab village --only heroBesideFire` reads his own pixels, drawn with him and without, its flames hidden, what its light alone burns). In the fog a fire stands still: its update is passed over, its moment of the loop held and none of it decoded for it (`inTheFog`), so the village's one campfire stops the loop.
