# What a hero costs to draw

From `src/entities/models/AGENTS.md`: how a hero's shadow, far detail and WebGPU draw are cut down. Paths are under `src/entities/models/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **A hero's shadow is cast as one** (`models/castAsOne.ts`, `Player.shadow`; #270): his rigid pieces of one front-sided material that never change shape (`castsAsOne`: no custom depth material, no alpha test, positions `StaticDrawUsage`) are merged into one skinned mesh the shadow map alone draws (`inShadowsOnly`), each piece a bone of its own that is the piece itself, drawn with `SKIN_DEPTH` (compiles nothing). It's made again only when a piece is shown or hidden or the quality cuts it from casting (`applyShadowDetail`), never per frame; a piece on a layer the camera doesn't see (the body through the eyes) is shrunk to nothing through its bone's inverse (three makes a skeleton's matrices again as it draws: zeroed before, the hidden body still cast), and the pieces cast alone are taken off for the draw and put back after (`aroundShadows` in `world/culling.ts`). The mesh hangs from the player's object, not the model: the first person sorts the model's meshes onto its layers once. A geometry whose positions change (a cloth, a skirt, a bowstring) must say so (`DynamicDrawUsage`), or it casts as it was merged; one that changes anyway is let go of to cast alone, with an error in dev. It saves 31 to 36 shadow draws a hero, about 0.1 ms of the ranger's and the mage's shadow pass in Chrome, the warrior's within the noise: the draws it saves were cheap ones. Check its look with `npm run lab -- shadows` (each weapon style, armoured and bare, each view); `?castAsOne=0` casts each piece alone.
- **A hero's shadow is cast from fewer triangles than he's drawn with** (`models/shadowDetail.ts`, in `castAsOne.ts`): a shadow map holds his outline alone, yet each draw of it sent every triangle of him, 134k a map on High, as many as the frame drew of him, into the sun's map and by night two lamps' too, whenever he moved (from play, the village's frame going from 400k to 900k triangles and back as he stood by the hall). Each caster's triangles are simplified once a geometry by three's meshoptimizer, an index of fewer triangles over the geometry's own vertices (`coarseIndex`: a fifth where the piece gives them up, by a grid of its points where its faces share no vertices, none within 6 mm of its surface lost): the mesh cast as one is merged of those, and a mesh that casts alone (a skinned part, a cloth) gives way for the draw to a twin of the same attributes and the coarse index, which the shadow map alone draws (`coarseTwin`; a cloth's moving points are its twin's too). A card cut by its picture (the hair) casts as it is. 134k to 65k a map for the mage, the ground's shadow and his own body's the same to the eye: `npm run lab -- shadows` draws the frame both ways (at most 0.2% of its pixels apart, by 40 levels; 2.6k pixels by 21 at the worst, the warrior's two-hander over the shoulder). **The error is in metres, not a share of the piece:** at 2% of a body's size the caster stood out of the mage's sleeves and staff through the eyes and shaded them, 23k pixels of the frame. The simplifier loads beside the game and the shadows are made again once it has; `?shadowDetail=1` (a harness: `castingAsOne.whole`) casts every triangle. WebGL's alone: on WebGPU a hero is drawn and cast as one by `drawAsOne.ts`. Count a moving frame with `npm run lab -- draws --village --moving`.
- **A hero seen from afar is drawn from fewer triangles** (`models/farDetail.ts`, `Player.farDetail`; from play, in the hall with three heroes in view, "1.3m not expected either"): on High he is about 140k at any distance, made for the views that stand beside him. Past 11 m from the camera (`FAR.out`: the view from above, a partner across the square, the hall's plinths as they're posed) each of his meshes draws a geometry of the same vertices under an index of fewer triangles (`farIndex` in `shadowDetail.ts`: three tenths where a piece gives them up, none further than 4 mm from its surface; no vertex moves, so colours, normals and skin are as they were), and its own again within 9 m (`FAR.back`: apart, so he never flips on a line). A swap of geometry, no program or material. From above the mage's meshes hold 107k triangles where 174k, the warrior's 120k of 184k, the ranger's 97k of 175k (every piece he can wear counted, shown or not), the frame within 0.05 to 0.26% of its pixels of him whole (`npm run lab -- village --only heroCoarseFromAfar`). A card cut by its picture (the hair, `hairCard` in `head.ts`: a ribbon of two vertices a step along its lock) is no simplifier's to cut, which can't tell what of it the picture leaves: from afar each three steps of a ribbon are drawn as one (`ribbonIndex`), its width, ends and picture's place kept and some of its bend lost. The warrior's hair was 53k triangles from afar, more than the rest of him: 19k now, the ranger's 39k to 13k; with it the mage's meshes from above hold 92k, the warrior's 96k, the ranger's 73k, the frame within 0.10 to 0.21% of its pixels of him whole. Pictured at 1.2 m, coarse beside whole, the hair's strands are a little more ragged and its shape the same. WebGL's alone; `?farDetail=0` draws every triangle at any distance.
- **A hero's fewer triangles are made in a worker** (`simplify.worker.ts`, sent a few ms a frame by `tickDetail` in `shadowDetail.ts`, from `farDetail`'s update; `simplifyIndex.ts` the simplification both run): made a few ms a frame on the page, a piece's one call (up to 15 ms, the hair's 32k triangles) held a return's first frames 12 to 16 ms (`lab lobbystart --profile`). A harness has them made on the page (`detailOnPage(true)` as the lab installs, `detailWork`), a frame's slice at a time, which its fixed clock steps the same on every run; `lab lobbystart` hands them to the worker as a player's page does, and a worker that fails hands what it was making back to the page. Before: simplifying a hero's meshes takes 45 to 55 ms of a desktop's CPU for his shadow and as much again for the frame from afar, and made as they were first asked for (in the shadow map's draw, #622) that was a frame held up for every hero built. Now an index asked for and not made is null, its geometry drawn whole meanwhile, and `shadowDetailVersion` moves on once none waits: the shadow cast as one and the far geometries are made again then. A hero is made again of his indices (his shadow cast as one, `castAsOne`'s `make`, and his far meshes, `farDetail`'s `find`) at a new `shadowDetailVersion` only while one of his own was still to come or every triangle has been asked for or let go since (`detailSettled`, `shadowsWhole`): the version moves on as any hero's are made, and each hero built behind the lobby had every hero built before him merge his shadow again, 14 ms a frame each. They're timed by `perfClock` (`core/perfClock.ts`): the lab's page clock stands still within a frame, so there a
frame makes all that's asked, unless a harness puts the browser's clock in its place (`lab lobbystart` does, as a
hall hero's 11 poses stood at rest each simplified a piece of him, and read as one frame of 60 to 80 ms).
- **On WebGPU a hero is drawn as one** (`models/drawAsOne.ts`, `Player.drawn`; `?drawAsOne=0` each part alone): three's WebGPU path costs about three times its WebGL path a draw, and a moving part writes all its uniforms at each one, so a hero (77 of the forest lobby's 134 scene draws, 22 of its 46 shadow draws) was most of why WebGPU's frame took twice WebGL's. His parts are merged by material into meshes standing still at the scene's origin, every vertex reading its joints' matrices off one texture (`jointedPosition`), and those meshes sit in a render bundle (`BundleGroup`), recorded once: his 31 draws are one command in the pass, and all his moving writes one texture. One mesh casts the merged parts' shadows; a cloth, whose positions change, still draws and casts alone. In turns under the profiler, the frame's JS read 15.6 and 19.7 ms against 20.5 and 23.3 (the machine loaded; compare in the same run). Merged as skinned meshes, half the draws gained 4%: three writes a skinned mesh's bones into its own uniforms at each draw. What's shown changes without a merge (each rebuilt the whole hero, 800 ms as the hall of heroes opened, and at every arrow a ranger took): a merged part hidden is shrunk by its gate (`jointGate`), a part shown that isn't merged draws alone, and only `SETTLED` views in a row of that merges again. A material on a merged mesh is a stand-in (`standInFor`) with an own property reading each of its base's: three keys a build by its material's own properties (inherited alone, materials with and without a map shared a build, its map null: the page never loaded), and a copy would miss a glow's pulse. A compile calls the scene's `onBeforeRender` and no `onAfterRender`, so what's set round a view (`aroundViews`) must not trust it was undone: believed, the hero's merged meshes stood where they first drew, his cloth walking off without him. A hand or a fur mesh draws alone (`userData.drawsAlone`): a hand's bones blend as dual quaternions and its maps read triplanar (`models/handNodes.ts`), and each fur mesh has its own lag. A merged mesh in the scene shows whatever its hero does: `view` looks as far up as the scene (the hall's heroes are kept out of it, and drew over the lobby).
- **A hero standing still is drawn as a few meshes** (`models/stillAsOne.ts`, WebGL; `?stillAsOne=0` part by part): the hall of heroes' dais heroes stand unposed while the camera is outside (`holdStill` in `game/hall.ts`), yet from the square the two were 133 of the frame's 439 draws, about 2.9 ms of a dev build's render CPU, for 0.03% of its pixels through the door. His shown parts are laid into meshes of his own space, one a set of parts drawn alike (`lookOf`: the material's kind, its shader's patch and key, every property it draws by and its kit looks; a skinned part's copy and the rigid parts' material it was copied from are one), each skinned vertex posed on the CPU as three's shader poses it (its bones' matrices blended by its weights, its normal by the same blend), and drawn in the parts' place, which are taken off every layer meanwhile (hidden, what hangs from one would go with it). Nothing compiles: a set with a rigid part draws as a plain mesh with that part's material, a set of skinned parts alone as a skinned mesh on one bone that never moves, with a skinned part's, each a program the hero already drew with. Left alone: a set of one, a see-through or shader material, a part with work of its own before its draw (the hands, the fur), one that casts a shadow, one whose material reads its own space (a worn item's glow, a hood's shade: `vFxPos` is the mesh's own position). The mage 77 draws to 48 and the warrior 58 to 37, the rest each its own look. It's made 1.5 ms a frame (about 17 ms of a desktop's CPU for both), each set drawn as it's made (its upload a frame apart), and made again as a part it holds changes (his far detail's geometry, gear shown or hidden), let go of as he's posed again: 3.4 MB of buffers (81k vertices, 58k triangles) while they stand still. `lab village --only heroesStillAsOne` draws the frame merged and part by part from the square, as seen and from a camera before the dais (0.0001%: a pixel; `--canary` lays the normals unturned, 1%).
