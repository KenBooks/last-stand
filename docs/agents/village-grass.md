# The village's grass

From `src/world/AGENTS.md`: the meadow's grass round the village (`meadow.ts`, `meadowNodes.ts`) and the quality levels' grass (`data/quality.ts`). The rest of the village is `village.md`. Paths are under `src/world/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

**The grass is drawn by the engine's look of it** (`src/engine/looks/meadow.ts`, ported line for line from `meadowNodes.ts`): three's WebGL path and three's WebGPU drawing of the scene were taken out: the Rust engine alone draws the game (`src/engine/AGENTS.md`, `src/engine/STATE.md`), and the meadow's GLSL with them (`FRAGMENT_COLOUR`, `VERTEX_BLADE`, `GRASS_SUN_IN_SHADOW`, `MATTE_DIRECT`, `ENV_RADIANCE_BLADE`, `drawnFromAbove` and the like below name it as it was). What the notes below say of the grass's look, its rings and tiles, its wind and what a level cuts is what the port keeps; a number measured on WebGL is that renderer's.

- **The grass's rings stay put as the view turns** (`focusOf` in `meadow.ts`): their middle is where the camera's sight meets the ground, from a sight near level as far as one 0.35 down would (`FOCUS_STEEP`): followed onto the ground as far as it went, a sight just under level threw it from 2 m to 24 m before him as the head bobbed through the eyes, and the dense ring with it ("grass a near circle that keeps updating in front of me"). `lab village` tilts the view through the eyes and fails a jump over 2 m a frame (it was 22).
- **In a whole view the grass covers all the meadow** (`core/wholeView.ts`, the developer mode's free camera; `drawnFromAbove`, `focusOf` and `wholeReach` in `meadow.ts`): never the one ring from above, which reaches 70 m from the game's own camera's sight and 35 m with it under 30 m, a circle of grass with the meadow bare past it from a camera flown off (#616). The rings' middle is where the camera's sight meets the ground, however far, kept on the meadow's square, and the last ring reaches the square's diagonal from there, whole to its end (`uBand`, `uS` and a count, its own put back as the view is let go): fine blades where a low camera looks, grass to the far corner from any height. `lab village --only freeCamWhole` reads the meadow 60 m and more from the hero: 98% of it shows blades, 23% unfixed.
- **The grass is drawn only where it grows** (`buildMeadow` in `meadow.ts`): the mask is read back once on the CPU into a grid of 0.5 m cells, any grass in each grown by 1.6 m (the blades read the mask aside and lean off their cells), and a ring's tile with none in it is never listed, as one outside the view isn't: over the square, the roads and what's built every blade was drawn as nothing. Measure with `npm run lab -- draws --village` (`--at`, `--facing`, `--view`).
- **The paving's curbs and the grass up to them** (from play, "no grass on paved road pls, if you want, lets have curb, grass will be up to curb"): a low stone curb along each road's edges and round the square (`curbs`, `curbLines`, `CURB` in `village.ts`; left out where its middle lies on other paving, a road's mouth; a road's edges run with its line's curve at its half width, `roadEdges`: wandering in and out every 2 m, the curbs were wavy, "nobody makes a curb this wavy"), and no road bends tighter than its half width and its curb (`ROADS`: the yard's turned 60 degrees in its last 1.6 m into its gate, its inner edge folded back over itself and the curb along it doubled over into slivers on the paving, "lets fix curb near training area"; now out of the gate and round on a 3 m arc; `lab village` reads each road's tightest bend), the mask cleared over the paving and the curbs alone, no worn band (`meadowMask`), each of the stair's road's ending square against its cheek (the road straight up the stair's middle, a touch wider than its steps, `ROADS`, and the square's edge held 1.5 m short of its foot, `SQUARE_NORTH`: narrower, the curb ran on across the front of the step's end, "curb would never transition into stairs"; aslant, or with the square's edge reaching the foot, one curb lay along its cheek; the stair's lamps stand behind them, one stood on the paving), each run's faces wound by the way it goes out (`curbGeometry`: wound alike, one curb of each road showed its underside and only a sliver was drawn; `lab village --only curbsFaceUp`), and in the blades' shader (`meadow.ts`) the warped mask is held under the mask itself (the warp wandered the grass up to 1.4 m onto the road) and a blade at the edge leans away from it. Each ring's blades are wider again by its cells' size against the first ring's (`uWider`): a ring of cells twice as wide has a quarter of the blades, and with blades only twice as wide it covered half the ground, the first ring a denser disc following the view ("a circle of different grass that moves when I move"). Drawn whole, those wide blades (four times the first ring's in the second, sixteen in the third) were a lighter, coarser carpet past the first ring's edge, a circle round the view as it moved (from play, "the circle of changing grass is still there, its about this far out, see where the far curb is, and it keeps moving as I'm moving"): each is cut into strands of the first ring's width as it's shaded, each pointed and cut at a height of its own (`COMBED`, `STRAND_CUT`), a program of its own (a discard in the first ring's would hold back the depth test for its dense blades), the count of blades and the ground covered as before; grown smoothly instead, each ring thinning and widening its blades with the distance, every blade past 4.5 m grew and shrank as he walked, and the far grass turned to leafy clumps. `lab village` draws the blades alone twice, the rings' middle moved 2 m between (`moveMeadowFocus`), and reads each metre's band's grain: drawn whole a band's changed 30 to 33%, cut 9%; the GPU's scene took no longer (`lab perf --village`); and the thin patches keep more of their blades, for grass "dense than this". The paving is shaded in broad patches over the world (`unrepeated`): its tile, a metre and a half, repeated its blotches in a grid ("texture tiling on the ground too obvious"). Its stones don't line up either ("still a little too obvious"): each map is read twice, shifted by two of six offsets a slow noise over the world picks, a patch a few metres across each, and blended over a narrow band (`unrep`); changed every half metre, or led by which stone stood taller, the stones were mush.
- **The grass's hand-over** (`RINGS`, `HAND_OVER`, `BLADE_GROWS` in `meadow.ts`; from play, "the circle of grass that keeps expanding/redrawing as I move", "grass needs fixing"): the first two rings are the same blades, at the same spots, the second with fewer segments, so they hand over at a sharp edge nothing can see (`AT_ONCE`: `lab village --only grassRingsGentle`'s bands from 5 to 13 m read 0%); only further out do blades come and go, each ring's across 40% of the one inside it, and a far ring's sheet stands whole through its band, each strand rising by a share of its own, all of them up before the band's middle (`STRANDS_RISE`; grown from nothing, a sheet up to 84 cm wide spread out from its middle at 20 m; risen across the whole band, it stood thinner and coarser than the grass either side). The worst band's light changed in a 0.5 m stride: 11.9% to 7.0%, at 17 to 18 m, the grain within 12% out to 40 m. It costs 240k triangles more (596k to 839k, at the square); the frame is on the next point. A slower blade (0.5) stood at half height through a band, thinner, and read worse. A blade seen edge on turns to the camera where it stands close to (`eyeOf`, `uEye`), and from above to a point along the view as far off as the sun: turned to where the camera stood, every blade in the frame moved with each of its steps, the meadow crawling as he walked (from play, "grass appearing/rerendering when I walk in top down view"; `--only grassStillFromAbove`, whose canary brings that back: 7.1% of a band's light in a stride, 0.0% now).
- **Low's grass is spread** (`grassSpread` in `data/quality.ts`, `setGrassSpread` in `meadow.ts`; from play, an S25 Edge's Profile at full res: the grass 21 ms of an 80 ms frame, and the owner's choice, "Keep cheaper grass"): on Low the rings' blades stand twice as far apart, each twice as wide, a quarter of them covering the ground as before; each ring's tiles stay as wide in metres (grown with the cells, the tiles listed past the view's edge shaded 67% of High's vertices from above) and a cut ring's sheets are cut into as many more strands (`uStrandsBy`: left as many, each sheet's dark root read from above as a comb). From above the one ring spreads whole too, its blades of one segment (`ABOVE_SPREAD_SEGS`) and its sheets cut into as many more strands, 15% of High's vertices: spread by the square root alone it was still 6.8 ms of an S25 Edge's 33 ms frame (from play, "ok but I want 60 fps", and Low may look plainer than High); its blades lie further apart, the ground showing between them. Uniforms, counts and a geometry: a quality switches it live, no program made. `lab village --only grassOnLow` counts the vertices the rings shade: 30% of High's from above, 26% over the shoulder, pictured; the phone's own time is the Profile's to say. **Very low spreads them four times** (a sixteenth of High's blades, each four times as wide; from play, a laptop's integrated Radeon in the village on Very low: 5 frames a second over the shoulder with the Resolution at a quarter each way, traces 44FC-MMSB and M501-CGAP, its GPU's time following the triangles drawn, not the pixels; `npm run lab -- draws --village --view third --boot verylow --count 0`: the grass 296k of the frame's 534k triangles, now 78k of 316k): 7% of High's vertices over the shoulder and 3% from above, held under a tenth by the same check; it reads as broad leaves more than blades, Very low's to be plain. What's left there, largest first: the trees' bark 55k, the palisade 19k.
- **The grass's tiles are small, listed in a texture** (`uTileMap`, `TILES` 32 a side on WebGL, `meadow.ts`, #604): a ring's tiles drawn were listed in a uniform array, 8 a side all a phone's programs had room for, so a far ring's tile was 9 to 21 m wide and every blade of a tile that touched the view or the ring's band was sent, to be dropped in the vertex shader (from play, 1.5M triangles facing a cottage's wall). Now a texel a tile, each 1 to 5 m wide, tried by blocks of 4 x 4 against the band and the view with `ROOT_STRAYS` (1 m) to spare: a blade's root stands that far off its cell, and tried by its cells alone a strip of thin grass went round the view (`lab village --only grassRingsGentle`: 13.2%). WebGPU's nodes keep the array, 8 a side.
- **No grass is drawn behind what's built** (`setMeadowOccluders`): a tile whose box's eight corners all lie behind one box from the camera (a cottage's walls, the terrace's block, the hall) isn't listed, over the shoulder and through the eyes alone, where what's built never thins. With the 8 x 8 tiles the same test culled nothing. The listing (each ring's `onBeforeRender`: `mayShow`, `hiddenBehind`) is about 0.37 ms of a frame on the lent 4080 (`lab perf --village --profile=mesh.onBeforeRender`, 515 ms over 1,380 frames). Tried and left out (#718): the eye put in each occluder's frame once a render, the grass tested before the occluders, and the tile map sent only when it changed. The triangles were the same in every phase, and it saved under a tenth of the listing, within the noise.
- **High's grass is thin blades cut from cards as they're shaded** (`grassTufts` in `data/quality.ts`, `TUFT*` in `meadow.ts`, #604; after solstice's grass, MIT, https://github.com/SamG-Coder/solstice, its `docs/meadow.jpg` the reference; from play, of ours, "this is high, looks terrible", "is that real grass to you ?"): a card bent along its middle (four triangles, a V from above) for every two cells each way (`TUFT_SPREAD`), its blades rooted all across it, two a bin of `TUFT_BINS`, each near upright with a lean and a curve of its own, as wide most of its length and then to a point, those of each half fanning a little from its middle (`TUFT_FAN`). Fanned from one crown, eight wide blades, a tuft was a wedge of paint strokes all leaning one way (three reviews of stills against the reference; from play, "our grass goes 45 degrees... his is not strictly 45, its more random"). None is thinner than a pixel (thinner, far tufts broke into pixels that came and went), and far off fewer of them are drawn, by the pixel's width where the tuft is rooted (`vDrop`): every blade widened to a pixel made a far tuft near solid, all bright tips, where the same tuft close to shows the dark between its blades ("very bright becomes very dark and back" on approach). **What a card costs is the pixels it covers, not its triangles:** a card a cell, at two thirds of main's triangles, took High's scene pass from 5.4 ms to 8.2 on a 4070 Ti, and three real blades a tuft took the frame to 3.6M triangles ("that's broken"). In the close views the rings' middle is the hero (`meadowFromAbove`'s `him`); from above near the ground (`ABOVE_NEAR`) High and Medium draw the first ring's own blades or tufts. The ground under the grass is dark and plain (`meadowGround`): lighter than the blades and streaked, it showed between the tufts as a woven carpet. Over the shoulder High's frame on the meadow is 533k triangles where main's was 2.05M (`lab draws --village --boot high --count 0`).
- **A number a tuft's blades are dealt by is rounded before it's hashed** (`cardSeed` in `FRAGMENT_COLOUR`): it reaches the fragment shader as a varying, the same at each of the card's corners, but not to the last bit once the card moves, and a hash makes another tuft of each such bit: every blade stood elsewhere from frame to frame in any wind at all, whatever its strength (from play, "grass is flickering now, I just stand idle, and it flickers"; before it, read as "flipping between dark/bright", it was chased through the wind's strength, the shadows and the colours). `lab village --only grassSteadyInWind` reads the grass from one frame to the next: 8.5% of its light changed a frame, 0.6 to 1.1% now, main's own blades 1.7 to 4.4%, no wind 0.5%. Any value hashed in a fragment shader that comes from a varying wants the same.
- **How a tuft is lit** (`bladeMaterial`): by a normal nine tenths up, turned to the side that faces up whichever way its card is wound (by `gl_FrontFacing` alone one face of every card was the dark one, half the tufts near black beside the rest, "shading on grass doesn't look right", "so this looks right to you ?"; found by drawing every blade one flat colour, then the two faces in two); matte, no specular (lit as a surface facing up and seen at a shallow angle each blade took the sky as a pale sheen, the lit grass a washed yellow beside the dark green of the shaded); keeping 60% of the sun in the sun's shadow (`GRASS_SUN_IN_SHADOW`: at none, under the trees' dappled shade one tuft stood three times as bright as the next); its shadow taken where it stands unblown. Its specular is never worked out where it'd be thrown away (`MATTE_DIRECT`, `ENV_RADIANCE_BLADE`: three's GGX for each light that reaches it, and its reading of the environment's reflection, both multiplied by `bladeSheen`'s nought after): the grass 25.4 to 25.7 ms of the 4080's scene pass at midnight to 24.6 (`lab scenecost --biomes village --screen 2880x1800@2 --hour 0`), its pixels as before. Its lighting is over half of what it costs: drawn unlit it was 10.7 ms of 23.2 at noon, 11.4 of 26.1 at midnight, the lights' loop and the sun's shadow, run on every blade a nearer one then covers. Its greens are near the reference's own, a blade in six straw.
- **The grass's wind comes in gusts** (`VERTEX_BLADE`; from play, "we should randomize wind too, but make it logical... like wind gusts"): patches of moving air a few metres across travel downwind, the grass near upright between them, the wind's own way wandering slowly over the meadow; a tuft's card sways mostly in its own plane and barely flutters. One steady wave pushing every blade the same way all the time read as grass combed at 45 degrees. Blades (Medium and under, and main's) keep the flutter they had.
