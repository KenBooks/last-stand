# Sounds and music

From `src/core/AGENTS.md`: how the synthesized sounds are mixed and voiced (`audio.ts`), each class's after the wizard's, and how the music plays (`music.ts`; the tracks themselves are `src/audio/music/AGENTS.md`). Paths are under `src/core/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **A sound's power is in its mids, not its level** (`audio.ts`): the master's compressor holds every effect under about 0.06 RMS, so a louder layer is only squeezed, and a laptop's speakers play almost nothing under 100 Hz (Void Lance's first drone, a 41 Hz sub, was silence there: from play, "not powerful enough"). Weight comes from what's above 150 Hz: driven saws (`drive`, a soft clipper), noise blasts and flanged roars (`flange`), and the shared hall (`space`). **A spell plays no notes over the music:** Void Lance's chimes, chord, choir and glittering bells played as a second tune in another key (from play, "the background music gets duplicated", "like coins falling"; no second track had started), and so was #413's rumble of saws on A under the beam, climbing an octave as the portal opened (a glissando, its 215 Hz overtone 9 dB over its noise). So Void Lance is noise alone, as raw arcana: a flanged roar, a driven pressure of dark noise, and the arcana crackling (`crackles`: sparse clicks, an electric discharge's spit, which the beam's sparks and Splintering Bolt's fizz are made of), each layer drifting at its own slow rate so a long hold doesn't tire (`lanceInhale` the portal, its spark's crackle going round the ears once a trace; `lanceBurst` the beam bursting out; `lanceDrone` the beam): held, 88% of it is above 150 Hz and no peak stands over 3 dB over its noise (`lab sound lance`). Render a sound offline to hear and measure it with `npm run lab -- sound` (WAV files in `tools/lab/out/sound`, its level, its share above 150 Hz, its most tonal peak, the nodes it made: `playInto` plays the effects into its `OfflineAudioContext`, and the throttles go by the context's own clock for it); and in the game, log `AudioBufferSourceNode.prototype.start` to see what a hold starts (a track is the one over 10 s long). The mage's other spells are built the same way, each the thing it shows: Splintering Bolt a small seeking orb thrown fast at every cast (`boltCast`: a snap, a push of dark noise, a zip falling as it leaves and its fizz trailing off, a little apart in colour and pan each time, as a held button casts one every 0.34 s; #413's thump, a sine falling from 260 Hz, was a kick drum three times a second over the music), and its burst on a foe (`boltBurst`: a pop, a spit of crackle, sparks and the two splits zipping off to either side; a split's own burst smaller, with neither; the shared `boltHit` was a tick the ranger's arrows make too). Held, its cast is about 9 dB over a bow's shot and its bursts some 10 dB over its cast; a cast and its three bursts make about 75 nodes (#413's 22, its bursts a tick each); Starfall's crystals falling and striking (`starfallFalling`, `starfallStrike`), Glacial Nova's crack, icy wind and ice forming as grains of noise (`novaBurst`: its three bell tones read as "a coin"), Maelstrom's burst and its spinning wind and fire for the storm's life (`maelstromBurst`, `maelstromSpin`, stopped as the storm's effect is disposed), and Veilstep's vanishing and forming where he lands (`veilstepPass`, noise alone: its first pop, a sine falling from 320 Hz, read as "a beep"; the dodge keeps `blink`, as `zap` stays shared with the other classes). The staffs' spells (#402) the same, after plain first takes (a filtered hiss and an oscillator each, and Lifethread's a chord and a note a second, a second tune; from play, "need better sound effects, pls match other spells/effects"): Cinderburst a heavy orb heaved (`cinderCast`: a spit, the heave, the flame's roar rising and falling, embers after it) and bursting (`cinderBlast`: a driven blow, a blast closing to dark, fire washing out to both sides, embers); Stormcall is an electric arc and thunder (from play, of takes made of `crackles` and a rumble wavering at a steady rate: "it doesnt sound like lightning at all", "tap should be like arc, and fully charged should be like thunder"; sparse clicks are a sparkler's fizz, and a steady waver an engine). Its buzz is `arcs`, a buffer of its own beside `crackles` (`voice`'s `arc`): sharp pulses of alternating sign about 110 a second, each gap drawn anew so it has no pitch, the arc breaking now and then. The charge (`stormGather`, from the skill's `charging`, stopped as it's let go or cut short) is that arc sputtering at the orb, slow and dull, then fast and harsh, static over it, from nothing (begun at a level, every tap had a blip before its spark); the arc leaping (`stormLeap`) a crack and the buzz quickening; the strike (`stormLand`) a full-band crack and the arc's buzz held on what it struck for a quarter of a second, bright over a dark growl of the same arc (shaped as a blow it was 20 dB down in a tenth of a second, a tick): a tap is that alone. A charged one adds the air tearing in a run of later, fainter, darker cracks, a blow, and thunder rolling away as booms at uneven times from side to side, for two seconds and more. `lab sound stormTaps,storm`: a tap -29 to -37 dB over 0.3 s, a full strike -18 falling through -27 to -40 over 1.5 s, no tonal peak over 6 dB; Lifethread held (`threadHeld`, stopped with the channel: warm air breathing through a slow flanger, a shimmer, sparse soft crackle as its motes, swelling a little once a second with its heal's number), gentle where the others strike. First takes with a 120 Hz driven sine at 0.42 and lowpassed blasts had 27% of Cinderburst above 150 Hz; with the blow smaller and higher and the blasts band-passed, 81%. `lab sound cinder,storm,thread`: the burst's loudest tenth -24 dB, as Starfall's strike and the nova's, the strike's -20, the charge held -28 (the lance's beam -26), Lifethread -32 to -36, no tonal peak over 5 dB. A held sound winds down, never cuts (from play, "not just have the sound abruptly end ... like a tune down"), but goes with what it's the sound of: Void Lance's beam is gone the frame it's let go, so its sound closes to dark in a fraction of a second (18 dB down in 0.2 s) with a falling whoosh, the last of its crackle and a soft whump (`lanceOut`), the hall ringing on after it (#413's sank two octaves over 1.5 s after the beam had gone), eased out by `setTargetAtTime`, as an exponential ramp to silence is 20 dB down a quarter of the way in and reads as a cut. A one-shot's `decay` (`env`) is the time to -80 dB, so a blow heard as a blow needs 0.3 s or more (the orb's first thump, at 0.16, was a click). A layer that starts late is silent until it does (`blowAt` sets its gain to nothing at the voice's start): a gain node is 1 until its first scheduled value, so Glacial Nova's 22 grains of ice, each meant for its moment over half a second, all played at full from the cast, and Veilstep's forming before he'd gone (the nova measured 5 times louder for it). An offline render's left and right levels over time catch it: a layer at full where it should be silent shows as a level, or a side, that the sound's own layers can't explain. The loading screen's exit has a sound of its own (`logoGust`, from `ui/loading.ts` as the wind starts, the plain fade none): the gust's body, rush and blown snow swept from the right ear to the left over the logo's letters, the swirl rising into the lobby's logo up on the left, a low sigh of air as it lands, timed to `ui/logoWind.ts`'s `SWEEP` and gather. It plays by itself, with nothing to press (from the owner's review of #422: "I dont like two new buttons ... it just needs to autoplay, and be less hammering on the brain"; #422's title menu waited for a click to be heard), wherever the browser lets sound play by then (`whenRunning`: the context running, or resumed within 0.4 s, as Chrome allows on a site visited often or the installed app); before a first visit's first click or key it's suspended and the gust isn't played, as a sound asked for then is heard at that click, in the lobby. It's soft, as the first sound a visit hears: rising over 0.7 s, its air opening no higher than 1.3 kHz, gently flanged, the swirl rising to 1.5 kHz and easing away, no pulses (#422's first take rushed to 3.2 kHz with a fizz of snow over 5 kHz flickering 11 times a second, and its swirl was cut off in 0.15 s: `lab sound logoGust` read its loudest half second -22 dB, its centroid 5.6 kHz with 74% of it above 2 kHz; now -34 dB, 1.5 kHz and 22%).
- **The warrior's sounds are steel, wood, earth and weight** (`audio.ts`, after the wizard's): every skill its own, built from shared pieces as the wizard's are from noise, so no notes. `bladeAir`: a blade's air, a band of noise rising and falling as the blade passes, lightly flanged, the hiss of its edge over it, crossing the ears the way the swing goes, timed to peak as the blow lands (a swing's sound is started from the skill's `charging` hook with the time left to its `fireAt`, as the hit comes from `cast`). `thump`: a blow's weight. `iron`: steel struck, narrow bands of noise (Q 12) at frequencies drawn anew at every blow, ringing for a third to two thirds of a second, so metal rings and never plays the same note twice (the lab's tonal peak stays 7.5 dB over its noise at most). `crunch`: armour and what's under it giving. `rumble`: the ground answering a great blow. `crack`, `grit`: a blow's first instant, stone and splinters. A weapon's `Heft` (a blade in each hand, a one-hander, a great weapon: `heftOf` in `combat/skills/cleave.ts`) sets each swing's and hit's pitch, length and weight. Steel Tempest is one held voice going round with the blades at the spin's own rate (it was a whoosh every 0.2 s), winding down when let go, as Power Strike's gathering sinks away if the cast is cut short. A prop struck plays `chip` by what it's made of (timber or stone), where every class's blow on one played `clang`, a triangle note at 1180 Hz. **A hit sounds wooden when its parts are short and high** (from play, "sounds like wooden swords and hits, I need a more grounded, strong effect"): the first take's steel was wide bands (Q 4.5) dying in a tenth of a second, a knock, and each blow's weight a lowpass starting at 2 kHz, gone in a quarter of a second. Grounded is steel ringing, a weight that lasts (`thump`'s second layer, the noise's floor driven hard), a crunch under it and the ground's rumble under the great blows. But the weight belongs in the mids: driving the noise's floor harder only fed the bass a laptop can't play (a cleave's share above 150 Hz fell from 98% to 69%), so what's under 120 Hz is cut after the drive and a blow's filter closes no lower than 110 Hz (back to 74 to 88%, the cleave's loudest half second up from -30 to -26 dB, Power Strike's from -23 to -19.5). The lab's scenes for them: `cleave`, `cleaveGreat`, `cleaveDual`, `fangs`, `crescent`, `shield`, `tempest`, `tempestGreat`, `bullRush`, `powerStrike`, `powerStrikeEarly`, `bellow`, `draught` (`lab sound`). **A blow of earth or a body rings no metal** (from play, on Bull Rush and Power Strike: "still sounds like its hitting a tincan"): their steel ringing on the stone and on a body, a plate rattle at 2.3 kHz as he ran and a narrow punch of noise at 330 to 360 Hz, driven, were a can struck. They're `whump` now, the noise under 450 Hz broad and driven hard, over `thump`, the dirt and stone lowered to 250 to 1500 Hz (their centroids 1.2 and 1.5 kHz down to 460 and 510 Hz); `iron` is for steel on steel and on a shield. But earth and weight alone are a muffled thud (from play, next: "should be a smash!"): a great blow needs its instant too, `smash`, the noise over 500 Hz driven hard and gone in a tenth of a second and stone breaking under it (their centroids up to 880 and 980 Hz, still under the first take's 1.2 to 1.5 kHz). Raising the shield plays nothing (from the owner's review of #527, "remove sound from raising the shield, remove visual effect also": it was a light tap of stone, and a gold ward stood before the hero); its blocks keep their sounds. The draught is a cork's pop, three glugs falling as they're swallowed and a dark rush of warmth, nothing over 900 Hz in the rush (from the same review, "needs work": its gulps rose a fifth each like a note, and a 4 kHz fizz hissed). A lab server is one per port, whatever its tree: another worktree's on the default port answered this one's `sound` with its own code ("the game has no bullRush"), so give a worktree's lab a port of its own (`LAB_PORT=5197 npm run lab -- sound ...`).
- **The ranger's sounds are wood, string, air and straw** (`audio.ts`, after the warrior's): every skill its own and each the thing it shows, noise alone, so no notes (before, a shot was a hiss and a triangle note falling from 180 Hz, the full draw two sine notes at 1560 and 2340 Hz, an arrow in a foe the wizard's old tick, `boltHit`, Quarry Mark a note gliding from 660 to 990 Hz and its refresh three notes of a chord; the draw, an arrow into the ground and the Strawman under blows played nothing of their own: `lab sound nockshot` on that game read a note 31 dB over its noise and its loudest half second -43 dB, some 15 dB under the other heroes'). Its pieces: `creak`, wood under strain, the crackle slowed until its clicks stand apart, quicker as the strain grows, through a narrow band; `knock`, wood struck, short and high (what made the warrior's first steel sound wooden is right here); `shiver`, something taut trembling, a band of noise whose level is shaken some tens of times a second at a rate drawn anew each time, so a string thrums and a shaft quivers without a pitch; `arrowAway`, an arrow's air leaping up as it passes the bow and falling as it goes; `stringLoose`, the string's slap, its thrum and the limbs' knock. A drawn shot (`bowDraw`, from the skill's `charging` hook, stopped as it's loosed or let down): the limbs creaking as the bow bends, settling to a faint creak now and then at full, some 10 dB under the shot, and for the archer's own ears alone, as the arrows' taking from the quiver and their nock on the string are (`nock`: the hero on this screen; a partner's shots and hits play as before, his creak doesn't). The full draw's sign is two ticks of wood and a breath of bright air (`bowReady`). Nockshot's loose grows with the draw; Briarflight's is a heavier slap and five arrows leaving across the ears; Lancewood Shot's the bow's heaviest, a shaft tearing the air, driven and with a punch of air at full, and through a foe it rips on (no quiver); Hailfletch's arrow climbs away, splits with a dry crack and splinters, and its shower whistles down to its landings. An arrow lands by what it meets (`arrowLand`: earth, wood, water; `ricochet` off stone, its head's tick two thin bands drawn anew, then the shaft clattering down), from `intoGround`, `intoProp` and `intoWater` in `skills/ranger.ts`, so every arrow that comes to rest is heard, a shower's too, thinned by the throttle (Hailfletch played a thud for three in ten). Quarry Mark is air drawn to a point and a snare's snap, its refresh arrows rattling back into the quiver and a breath; the Strawman is earth breaking, straw rustling (dense bright crackle), its coat's cloth flapping and its pole knocking, each blow on it a thud on its sack and a crunch of straw (`strawHit`, where it played the shared `thud`), and it rustles back into the ground. **A gain that rises from nothing is silent until its end** (`swellAt`'s exponential ramp from 0.0001: over more than a tenth of a second all but the last of it is under -30 dB): the shower's whistle and the mark's breath in were first heard only as they ended, so what must be heard coming starts at a level (`riseAt`). The first take's creak was as loud as the shot it led to (-30 dB against -27). The lab's scenes: `nockshot`, `nockshotQuick`, `nockshotHeld`, `briar`, `lancewood`, `lancewoodShort`, `hail`, `rangerMany` (two rangers at once, the loudest case: peak -6.6 dB, no clip), `quarry`, `strawman`, `glance`, `arrowLands` (`lab sound`): the loudest half second -23.6 dB (a full Lancewood Shot) to -34, a Nockshot -31.5, 89 to 95% above 150 Hz, no tonal peak over 8.5 dB. Which sounds a held key plays, when, and that the creak stops, is `lab heard` (`tools/lab/COMMANDS.md`).
- **The ambience** (#745, `ambience.ts`, from `ambienceWanted` in `main.ts` each frame beside the music): four recordings (`src/audio/ambience/`): the village's forest by day (birds, leaves) and by night (crickets, an owl), the fountain's water, and a fire burning. They replaced sounds made of noise, from review: "Sounds need a lot of work, the water does not sound like water, and the crackling does not sound like crackling. Use attached sounds instead" (and before that, the noise's crickets were "super annoying"; the wind was taken out, "Wind will be messing with people and not let them enjoy the game"). Each is fetched the first time it's wanted (never with the game, and kept out of the install: `ON_DEMAND` in `scripts/pwa.ts`), decoded and made a seamless loop (`seamless`: its faded ends cut, `fadedEnds`, its last 1.5 s blended into its first at equal power, then evened to an RMS of 0.1), looped by a buffer source over its whole length, sample-exact ("you should not hear anything when the audio restarts"). The forest's two ease in and out over an hour and a half either side of 6:30 and 19:00 (`dayShare`), heard in the village's lobby alone, at a fifth in a house's room or the hall of heroes. The fountain is full within 3 m of its middle and gone past 16, outdoors alone. Every fire built (`FIRES` in `world/fire.ts`: the battlegrounds' braziers and bonfires in their runs, the village's campfire, the houses' hearths, the hall of heroes' torches) is heard while what it was built into is shown, the nearest three at once (`firesHeard` in `world/firesHeard.ts`, `FIRE_VOICES`), each full within 2.5 m and gone past 14, from the hero (from the camera in the hall); the three voices start apart in the loop so two never play the same moment. Each layer is panned to its side of the view and its level eased (0.8 s) to what the frame asks. Well under the music at the default volumes (from Kenneth: "Sounds are way too loud, can barely hear the music": the music was 4 to 8 dB over it, LUFS against LUFS; every layer lowered 8 dB, it's 12 to 16 over it: `lab village --only ambienceUnderMusic`). `lab sound ambienceDay,ambienceNight,ambienceFire,ambienceFountain`: the loudest half second -44.6 dB by day, -45.7 by night, -42.9 at the fountain, -39.5 beside the campfire. `lab village --only ambienceHeard` reads the levels asked at noon and midnight on the square, beside the fire and the fountain and inside the library by its hearth, and counts every place's fires; `ambienceSeams` reads each loop across its seam; `lab fire` that a battleground's fire is heard 4.5 m off it in a run. Not yet: an Ambience slider of its own (the effects' sets it).
- **A press that casts nothing is heard** (#572, `sfx.denied`, `deniedKnock` and `deniedFizzle` in `audio.ts`; `Player.denied`): a skill pressed while it cools, or with nothing to cast at, knocks (two muted knocks of broad noise round 500 Hz, the second softer and lower 75 ms on, "not yet"), and one pressed short of energy fizzles (a breath of noise falling from 2.6 kHz to 520 Hz with a sputter of crackle, a spark that won't take). Noise alone, dry and short (gone in about 0.2 s), as a spell plays no notes over the music. Only a press is heard: a key held down is tried every frame and casts once the skill is ready, so it knocks once. At most one every 0.3 s of the game's time (`DENIED_EVERY` in `entities/player.ts`, by `G.time`, not the audio clock, so it pauses with the game and the lab can check it), this game's own hero's alone (a partner's copy runs no input) and never in free casting. `lab sound deniedCooldown,deniedEnergy,deniedMashed`: the knock's and the fizzle's loudest half second -56 and -49 dB (a bow's shot and a swing -56, a Splintering Bolt cast -36), 99 to 100% above 150 Hz, no tonal peak over 10 dB (5.5 over four knocks); `lab denied` checks when it plays.
- **Music** (`core/music.ts`, `data/music.ts`): each biome has a track for its lobby and one for its waves' fights (`BiomeDef.music`): the Thornwood's lobby plays Quiet Beginnings and its fights Sunlit Forest Battle, the crypt's Shadowed Forest Dusk and Crypt Motif. The village plays the lobby track of its hour (the Thornwood's by day, the crypt's from dusk: `biomeOfHour`, `musicWanted` in `main.ts`), and a biome's lobby track plays in its run wherever there's no fight: the countdowns, the choice after a wave, the summary. The battle track comes in as each wave's fight starts (`G.run.phase === 'fighting'`) and fades out once the wave is won, the lobby's coming back as it goes (`FADES`: the lobby's in over 3 s and out over 1.2, the battle's in over 1 and out over 2.5). Every track comes back where it was left (the lobby's between wave 1 and 2 where wave 1's fight left it, the battle's on from wave to wave), from its top only once it was let go: a new run's first wave, the village's hour turning from one to the other, which crossfades the two lobbies'. Each track is a loop of its piece made by `npm run music -- make` (`tools/music/README.md`, which says how each was cut: Crypt Motif has no repeats, and loops whole): the piece up to where it comes back, a crossfade into the loop's start and a second more of it, looped from just past the crossfade to as far past the loop's end (`TRACKS`' `loop`), so both sides of the seam are the same samples whatever delay a decoder leaves at the file's start (an audio element's loop left a gap of the MP3's padding). MP3s at 44.1 kHz, LAME's VBR quality 5, about 110 kbps (Opus would be smaller, but iOS Safari plays it unreliably), 4.9 MB for the four. Each is fetched whole and decoded when it's first wanted (a run's battle track as the run starts, so its first wave doesn't wait; through a run both its tracks stay decoded), looped by a buffer source and faded by a gain node of its own (iOS ignores an element's own volume) through the effects' WebAudio context, and its decoded samples (20 MB a minute) are let go once it's silent and wanted nowhere. The music is asked to start as the lobby shows (a browser lets it start before the page's first click or key only where it allows autoplay, as Chrome does on a site visited often or an installed app; elsewhere that first gesture starts it), and stops in a hidden tab, playing on from there when it comes back. Measure with `npm run lab -- music` (`--canary`): each biome's lobby, a run's countdowns, fights and wins, a hidden tab and the bank back, judged by what reaches WebAudio and what each track's output carries.
