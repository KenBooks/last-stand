# The pause menu, its overlays and cloud saves

From `src/ui/AGENTS.md`: the pause menu's tabs and what they open, the performance overlay, the battle log, photo mode, the bug report and cloud saves. Paths are under `src/ui/` unless they're given whole.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **The pause menu opens on the tab chosen last** (`choosePauseTab` in `menus.ts`, the cookie `last-stand-pause-tab`; from play: "default to tab in pause screen that was open before... not always controls... persists between reloads"), Controls until a tab is chosen. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states pauseTabKept`.
- **The pause menu has no links to what the village's places open** (from the owner, of Change hero, Play together and How to play along its foot: "pls remove these links, they are not supposed to be there"): the hall of heroes, the campfire and the notice board are walked to and used (E), clicked or tapped from anywhere, or picked on the map (M, the touch Map button), which walks him there and uses it. Its foot keeps Install alone (`.pb-links`), where the browser can install the game. The lab opens the hero selection screen by `askForHeroes` (`tools/lab/page/uiHeroes.ts`: what the dais asks), and `lab ui --states pauseLobby` pictures the menu.
- **The bug report's form** (`bugReport.ts`, its text `bugReportText.ts`, `#bug-report`, `styles/_bugReport.scss`; the kinds and the limits `data/bugReport.ts`; from play: "way for users to report bugs... some way of categorizing the issue"): Report a bug stands at the pause menu's foot beside the build's status (`.pb-build-line`: on the build's own line, it adds no row to a phone held sideways), in the lobby and in a run. The form over whatever is up (the pause menu under it out of sight, `:has`): one kind picked of eleven a player tells apart at a glance (where the trouble shows, not where its code lies: Gameplay, Looks wrong, Slow or freezing, Sound, Controls, Playing together, Menus and text, Saves, Crash or stuck, Idea, Something else), what happened, what was expected if they like, and a picture of the game's view as the form opened (`bugReportAfterRender`, called by `main.ts` after the frame's render: the canvas keeps its pixels only within that task; shrunk to 1280 px, a JPEG, made again at lower qualities till it's under the server's 600 KB), which a tick leaves out. The game adds a line a fact (`reportFacts`): where the player was (the screen, a run's wave and what it was doing, the biome), the hero, solo or how large the party, the build, the graphics, the screen, the device (a trace's own `device()` and `settings()`, `perf/upload.ts`: gathered once). Never a name tag, a room's code, a save or an item; the picture is the canvas, so in a room it shows the name tags drawn over the frame, which the form says. "See everything in the report" shows the text whole, as text (`textContent`), and nothing leaves until a button is pressed. **Send goes to our own server alone** (`bugReportSend.ts`: the traces service's `POST /traces/reports`, `server/traces/AGENTS.md`'s Bug reports, where every limit is listed; never GitHub, from the owner: "Lets not involve Github in client issues, we'd have a mess"): the kind's id, what was typed, the facts and the picture if ticked, as JSON in a plain request (no preflight, no cookies; the offline worker answers GETs alone). Sending says so with Send held off; sent, the last page gives the report's code; refused or unreached, the form stays as it was, says why in the player's words (a 429's `Retry-After` as when to try again) and Send becomes Try again; offline it says so before trying. Copy report puts the text on the clipboard and keeps the form, and its last page offers the picture to copy or save, as the clipboard holds one or the other. Solo the game stops under it and in co-op it goes on, the hero idle, as the pause menu does (`openReport` in `main.ts`, which also holds the game when the form was opened with no pause menu under it); its keys are its own (a field's never reach the game, `core/input.ts`'s `typing`; Esc closes it, from a field by the form's own listener, and `handleGlobalKeys` gives it every key first). What's typed is kept while the page lives, till a report is sent. On a short screen (a phone held sideways, however narrow) the kinds go across the whole form in two rows of six (the eleventh across two places, so no row ends short), their hint on a line of its own under them (beside their label, half the form's width wrapped it), and what happened down the left beside the rest (`_bugReport.scss`): in the narrow window's one column, a 640x360 phone's touch form was 778 px of content in 561, its buttons under the window's foot. Its box is zoomed by `--ui`, so on touch its buttons and fields are sized in real css px over `--ui` (kinds 36, buttons 40, fields' text 16: under that a phone zooms the page into the field), and a field focused on touch keeps the form over the keyboard (`aboveKeyboard.ts`, shared with the cloud saves' code field). Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states bugReport,bugReportTouch` at each screen `src/ui/AGENTS.md` lists (`page/uiBugReport.ts`: the form within the window and not scrolling, every kind and control found at its middle, the picture taken and not blank, a report made by the mouse from the lobby, failed once by the server and sent again to a reports service the lab starts, read back with its token, and filled in by a finger over a run; nothing of the page over the form; `--canary` makes the box twice the window's height, lays a panel over the touch form and drops the picture on its way, which the read-back must find). Run it at 640x360@3 too: the narrowest phone held sideways.
- **The pause menu has four tabs: Controls, Settings, Performance and Guide** (`PAUSE_TABS` in `menus.ts`; Guide's doors open the guide over the menu, `guide/AGENTS.md`). Performance holds what changes or reports how fast the game runs (Graphics, Vsync, Renderer, Slow-game reports, the performance and network stats) and the profiler (`profileTab.ts`, `src/perf/AGENTS.md`): while it runs every panel is hidden (`body.profiling`, `styles/_perfAsk.scss`), the scene alone shown with a pane at the top (`#profiling`: the part being timed, a bar, the share done and the seconds left, Cancel), and closing the menu (Esc) cancels it (`cancelProfile` in `openPause`); its five costliest parts are then listed under its button. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states pausePerformance,profiling --screens 832x384@3.75,1280x720@1`.
- **Gear glow** (the pause menu's Settings, `setGearGlow` in `core/materials.ts`): worn gear's rarity glow on or off, switched live (a uniform's value: nothing compiles), kept in a cookie once the player chooses. Until then the quality level decides (`QualityPreset.gearGlow`): on, but off at Very low (from play: "turn off gear glow automatically at very low, could be toggled on in settings if user prefers it on"), the buttons showing which is in effect. `npm run lab -- glow` presses Off and On; `lab ui --states pauseSettings --screens 832x384@3.75,1280x720@1` holds the tab unscrolled.
- **The volumes** (the pause menu's Settings, `initVolumeOptions` in `menus.ts`, #408): Master, Music and Effects, a slider each with its share after it, set and saved (`last-stand-volume`) as it moves, heard at once (`setVolumes` in `core/audio.ts`); the Effects slider plays a click as it's let go, to hear its level by. They stand atop the Settings tab's second column, which fits a phone held sideways without scrolling (`npm run lab -- ui --states pauseSettings --screens 832x384@3.75,1280x720@1`), and a slider is dragged by a finger as by the mouse. HDR's levels and the volumes share their look (`.opt-levels`, `.opt-level` in `styles/_modals.scss`). Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states pauseVolume` (the Music slider clicked: shown, saved and heard; `--canary` loses the setting).
- **The pause menu's build and its status share a line on a short screen** (`.pb-build`, `styles/_modals.scss`): a line each, the status ("Latest version", `buildStatus.ts`) pushed the pages' cell 10 px under the box's foot on a phone held sideways, and the Controls tab scrolled. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states pause,pauseSettings,pausePerformance --screens 832x384@2.8125,1280x720@1`.
- **Abandon run is held, never clicked** (`holdButton.ts`, the pause menu): a stray click or tap lost a run's spoils, so it acts only once held 2 s (the mouse, a finger, or Enter or Space while focused), a fill running across it as it's held; let go early, it says "Hold to abandon" over its label. Its cost is in its tooltip. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states pauseAbandon` (a click, a second's hold and a full one; `--canary` makes the hold 0 ms).
- **Photo mode** (`photo.ts`, `#photo` in `index.html`, `styles/_photo.scss`, its look `core/photoLook.ts`; from the owner: "picture mode, where ui is removed and you can adjust time of day... where you press button it takes the picture on its own and saves on pc"): P or the pause menu's Photo mode (Settings tab) opens it, in the lobby or between a run's waves (never during a fight, `photoAllowed`: the pause menu's button is disabled then and says why, and a wave begun while it is open, as in co-op, closes it; from play: "make unavailable during battles, in village, and between waves it works though"), never over the hero selection screen, the save slots, the profiler, the guide or a dialog (a talk, Odo's trade, a co-op trade, Play together, How to play: a talk frames the camera itself, and a dialog's state would wait unseen under it). Every other part of the page is hidden (`body.photo`) and the world's overlays are left out of the frame (`showOverlays` in `core/renderer.ts`); solo the game stands frozen as the pause menu holds it, in co-op it can't and the world goes on, the hero idle (the panel says so). Its camera turns round the hero's chest: a drag on its pad turns it, a right drag or Shift moves it, the wheel or a pinch zooms (Shift: the lens), WASD and Q/E fly it, Z/C tilt it, R recentres; it stays within 70 m of where he stood in the village, inside a battleground's wall and over the ground; in a house's room what it turns round stays inside the walls and the camera at most 6 m beyond them (`keepInRoom`: past them there's only the dark, as the room is all that's drawn), the walls it stands beyond cut away as the game's own camera has them (`Room.cutAway`, from its own place: the game's camera stood inside, and the wall stayed between). The look (exposure, bloom, contrast, saturation, a filter, the vignette, a focus blur as a tilt-shift) is uniforms of the grade the boot compiled (`uPhoto`'s branch, bloom's strength), so a slider compiles nothing; WebGPU's grade has no port of it, its sliders greyed out there with why. The time of day is the village's alone (greyed out in the battlegrounds and in a house, which keep their own light). Hide hero moves his meshes to the hidden layer (never `visible`, never a light: a light gone changes the light count and recompiles every program). Space or Take photo saves the next frame as the post chain drew it (`frameShot`, in the frame's own task: no `preserveDrawingBuffer`), graded for an SDR screen, at the canvas's pixels, with the letterbox's bars if shown, as a PNG download named by the local time (`last-stand-2026-11-15-213005.png`). H hides the panel for the shot, Esc or Leave puts back everything it changed (the hour, the sight's fog, the look, the hero's layers) and the pause menu if it came from it. On an upright screen the panel stands along the foot, on a short one it's tighter and its keys' line is left out. Check with `npm run lab -- photo` (and `--canary`).
- **The battle log** (`battleLog.ts`, "Show battle log" in the pause menu): every hit the local hero deals, under the skill it came from (`HitOpts.skill`: a dash's step, a timer or a channel's tick can't be told from what is being cast), and every blow he takes, under the foe and its attack (`hurtPlayer`'s attacker), with what his armor, shield and ward stopped. They come through combat/damage's log sink, null while it's off or paused (its panel's Pause: nothing listed until it goes on, so what it shows stays to be read), so off it costs nothing. A line names the hero alone on his side and counts the foes on theirs (it once counted him: "Mossback strike ▸ You ×3"), and says what stopped each part of a blow ("stopped by armor", "resisted", "blocked by shield", "absorbed by ward": a bare "17 armor" wasn't clear). A burst's hits (a channel's ticks, a held attack's blows) join one line while they keep coming. It's drawn from the main loop at most every 0.1 s, on a layer of its own. The overlays down the left (`#diag`: it, the performance and network stats) keep clear of the one panel a short screen's lobby shows (`clearOverlays` in `menus.ts`, once a frame while one is shown, as drawn, so they follow a panel as it slides: they lay over it). On touch they sit under the touch HUD's corner (the pause and scores buttons, the bars, the buffs), on the move stick's pad, so they let a touch through to it: the log but for its links, and the whole performance overlay (a button the whole panel round, it took a third of the move area's width on the owner's phone: "they only activate in a small area now, used to be better"). A quick still tap on the overlay copies its report, through the stick's pad (`copyPerfReportAt`, from `touch.ts`'s `TAP_NEAR` and `TAP_MS`): its frame rate a button of its own, its click came one tap in two, and a tap on the rest of the panel copied nothing ("I still can't copy the report by tapping it"). Where the pad is hidden (the lobby's panels shown, a menu open) the panel takes its own taps (`_perf.scss`). Check with `npm run lab -- touchpad --screen 832x384@3.75` (every point of the move area starting the stick, with the overlays shown and without). Check with `npm run lab -- log` (every hit listed as it landed, the panel showing every line) and `npm run lab -- ui --states logLobby,log,logTouch` with a phone's `--screen`. (It came from a Bull Rush that seemed to deal nothing: its number showed a moment after a charged Power Strike's, and was missed.)
- **The performance overlay has three sizes** (the pause menu's Performance stats, Off | Mini | Full: `setPerfHudSize` in `perfHud.ts`, `#opt-perf`): Full is all of it; Mini is the frame rate and its graph alone, in a row (`#perf.mini` in `styles/_perf.scss`), about a tenth of Full's room (from play: "minimize it to only show fps, so I have more screen"; the owner: "should still show the graph in mini, cause we want to see the trend"). Switched live, the window of frames kept between Mini and Full so the graph goes on, and kept in the cookie `last-stand-perf-hud` (`off`, `mini`, `full`; the `1` the builds before Mini kept for shown reads as Full, and the stalls tool still sets it). Mini copies the report as Full does, by a click, or on touch a tap, and its hint shows only to say it was copied (`.said`). A harness's `setPerfHud(on)` shows it whole. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states perfMini,pausePerformance` at each screen `src/ui/AGENTS.md` lists (Mini pressed with the mouse in the Performance tab: its parts, its room, its cookie; `--canary` loses the cookie), and `buttonsRunTouch` and `lab touchpad` hold Full clear of the touch buttons and the stick (`touchpad` taps the frame rate and the graph, each to copy a report). The overlay's draws and triangles are the larger of the last two frames': on Very low the sun's shadow is drawn every other frame, and the phone's read 392k triangles, then 132k, in turn.
- **The graph's points are half seconds, its scale the highest of them** (`computeStats` in `perfHud.ts`): the first, partial one is rated by the time its frames took, from the first frame's start, and only once that's half a bucket. Rated from the first frame's end, a redraw that fell just past a bucket's edge read one frame over a fraction of a millisecond, and the scale at the graph's top (which Mini shows beside its graph) read 2500 on the owner's 144 Hz screen, the line flattened under it, for 15 s after the overlay was shown (#462); the frames themselves were as ever. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states perfGraph` (shown afresh with its first frame every share of a frame long, as a real screen's frames jitter, the frame rate and the scale read after every frame against the frames' own rate: main's read 102420 on the lab's 60 fps; `--canary` runs a burst of frames 1 ms apart, a real rise, which must be read).
- **The perf overlay's clock** (`perfHud.ts`): the durations it reports (the update by stage, the render) are timed by `perfClock.now`, which a harness swaps for the browser's own clock (`npm run lab -- perf`: the lab's page clock stands still within a frame), while its window and graph run on the frames' timestamps. The update's stages are named where `main.ts` laps them (`perfLap`): the village's own work (its lamps, the hall's standing heroes, the map) is `village`, apart from the heroes' own updates (`player`) and the hall of heroes' screen (`hall`), as a report from the hall couldn't tell them apart. `lastPerfFrame()` and `takeGpuFrames()` give a harness each frame as the overlay summed it; the overlay's draw count is the GL draws (`renderer.info`), so a mesh three sets up and never sends to the GPU (an instanced mesh of none) isn't in it. Its report says, a 0.5 s bucket at a time beside the frames a second, our frame's time, how late it started and the GPU's time for our draws, says whether the fight's and the heroes' programs are warm and whether a warm-up runs, marks a worst frame drawn while one ran (`(warming)`), and lists the browser's long frames (50 ms+, Chrome's Long Animation Frames API) with what the main thread did in each (the tasks before its render, the frame callbacks, style and layout, the longest script by what ran it): a fall with ours and the GPU's steady and the frames late, and no long frame, was held up outside the page (its GPU's process, the machine). It also lists the boot's long frames, to the loading screen's leaving, each at its time from the page's start and the loading step showing then (a phone's loading froze 1 to 2 s at a time with the overlay hidden, long past its window by the time it showed; `watchBootFrames`, buffered from the page's start). On a short screen the overlay scrolls within it, its Profile button high in it (a phone with the browser's bars, 298 css px tall, put its foot under the screen's). `perfReport()` gives a harness the report (`npm run lab -- perf` checks it, the boot's frames too).
- **Cloud saves on the save slots screen** (`cloudSaves.ts`, `#saves .sv-cloud`, `#cloud-dialog`; the sync is `loot/cloud.ts`):
  - The bar under the title. Signed out: sign in with a passkey, New account, Use a sign-in code. Signed in: slot 1's state and Sync now, with the account's actions under Account, a dropdown (a chevron, closed by a press anywhere outside it or by Esc): Sign in elsewhere (a code; a preview's signs in on previews alone), Delete account on production alone, Copy my production heroes here on a preview. Add a passkey shows where the server takes one: on production, and on a preview only in the 10 minutes after a code signed this device in (`passkeyWindow`); shown on a preview where it was refused, it showed the server's bare reply.
  - The dialogs: which slot becomes the cloud save; the cloud's save coming down, naming the slot pushed out before anything changes; a hero changed in two places, both sides side by side; a code; deleting. A refusal is said in a sentence of what to do (`failure`), never the server's own words.
  - Slot 1 wears the cloud's mark. Google sign-in/link/unlink controls and the linked Google method carry its logo, including while the current session method is unknown. The identity block sits at the right of the slot header beneath Cloud; Privacy stays grouped with the last cloud action so it never wraps alone. Its identity block shows the current sign-in method separately from linked methods and sync status (#539). A Google session shows the Google logo and verified email, escaped as text and wrapped within its pane; passkey/code sessions never borrow that identity. Older Google sessions ask for another sign-in to show email. `lab ui --states cloudIdentity` checks the account snapshots at the UI sizes; its canary removes the email. Slots rearranged glide from where their save was to where it is now (`moveSlots` in `saves.ts`: transforms alone, slot 1's rising in last, the one pushed out falling away). With reduced motion they simply change.
  - Esc closes a dialog, then the Account menu, before the screen.
  - Google (#503): Sign in with Google beside the passkeys, and Link or Unlink Google in the Account menu on production, each shown only when the service says Google is on (`showIfGoogle`). Back from Google, the screen opens on choosing the cloud save, or says what went wrong (`showGoogleLanding`, `GOOGLE_ERRORS`).
  - A Google link that is already held by another production account may offer to move it to the current account after the same tab's OAuth proof. The dialog names whether that old account has preview saves or a protected production save, and does not promise to merge saves. A production save on a Google-only old account blocks moving until a passkey is added there.
  - On production, Account offers a one-time copy code. On a preview, Copy my production heroes here takes that code from a separate production sign-in and replaces the preview's cloud save. Its field follows the sign-in code's keyboard handling; a preview session never reads production's save by itself. The Account menu scrolls on short screens because its extra action cannot push the last button off a landscape phone.
  - **A field typed into on a phone** (the sign-in code): the field comes first, and once it has the focus on touch the dialog moves to the top of what the keyboard leaves (`aboveKeyboard`, `#cloud-dialog.typing`, as tall as the visual viewport) and stays there until it closes. A keyboard in full screen lies over the page without shrinking anything, so the top is what's seen. The dialog never moves back on blur, and its backdrop closes it only when pressed and let go there: either way a tap landed where the dialog had moved from (the field's tap closed the dialog, Sign in's missed), both found by `lab cloud`.
  - The privacy page is `public/privacy.html`, linked from the bar.
  - Check with `npm run lab -- cloud`.
