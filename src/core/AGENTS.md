# `src/core`

- `inputDelivery.ts` aggregates key arrival/handler/editing counts, pointer presses, pointer movements and maximum DOM delivery delays once a second during manual recording. Movement without clicks must be counted: windowed trace 65SQ-60GS showed steady frames while the player reported a roughly 5 FPS cursor for ten seconds, without clicking or testing keys. Capture is installed before UI key interceptors. A zero-arrival second with live frames differs from captured keys swallowed before the game's handler. No key codes, coordinates, text or event objects are retained. DOM timestamps cannot measure time spent in a native queue before the browser creates the event.

- `keyDelivery.ts` measures DOM dispatch delay from a valid monotonic timestamp, not the key's value. Ignore missing, future, epoch and non-finite stamps. `main.ts` records at most one delayed-delivery mark a second, plus focus tags; no key codes or typed text enter traces. It cannot alone detect a native queue that delays the event before that timestamp exists; compare manual arrival counts as well.

The engine under the game.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

**The game's frame is drawn by the Rust engine alone** (`src/engine/AGENTS.md`, `src/engine/STATE.md`): three's WebGL path and three's WebGPU drawing of the scene were taken out, and three's WebGPURenderer (`webgpu.ts`, `renderer.ts`) is the engine's host (the labels' and the outline's canvas). What this file says of three's programs, its lit chunks' patches, its passes (the post chain, bloom, the shadows' maps, the ghosts' count) and of WebGL against WebGPU is history, kept for what was measured: the code of it left in these modules is being taken out (grep for a name before relying on it; the lit chunks' patches still serve the guide's portraits' WebGL renderer). The engine has its own of each (its post chain, its lights' loop, its shadows, its levels), and a rule below holds for it only where `src/engine/AGENTS.md` says the same. The lab has no `--renderer`: a command runs on the engine, and `--ab` against a commit is the comparison. Live as written: the grade's numbers, the views, the canvas's fit, the quality's levels and Auto, the frames' pace, input, the sounds, the maps and textures.

- **Auto judges steady play alone, and the GPU's part of it** (`loadingFrame`, `noteGpuFrame` in `quality.ts`): a frame of a warm-up, of the fight's loading bar (`isReadying`), of the hall of heroes or after a program compiled at a draw is held (`holdQuality`, a settle after); where a timer query gives the GPU's own time a frame (WebGL's `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`, WebGPU's timestamps: `ui/perfHud.ts` hands each frame's on), a window under the target whose GPU frames' middle is within `gpuShare` (0.8) of the target's frame steps nothing and keeps a trial, as no lower level makes a CPU-bound or loading frame faster. Without GPU times (most phones, Safari) it judges the frames as before. A step down is tried again once a level after `retry` (30 s) of play. From play, "it sometimes settles on low/med on a powerful machine": the owner's 4070 Ti tried High at 5.8 s of a boot and left it at 20.2 s for Medium, its GPU 6 to 12 ms a frame, its frames held to 50 to 70 a second by the CPU (13 to 16 ms of ours a frame) and the frames round the fight's loading bar (trace P307-C0HE); played through Auto as main judged, a first visit of the same frames settled on Low. `lab auto --canary` plays both traces.
- Auto may climb through multiple successful trials in one visit, including Low→Medium→High and Very low→Low→Medium→High. Keeping a trial schedules the next; a failed trial returns to the last proven level and keeps its backoff. Choosing Auto also permits the climb after its initial settle. Do not restore the one-level cap: Low startup otherwise strands capable devices at Medium (`lab auto --canary --cold`).

The perf report and the traces say `vsync off`, and its frames never set the display's rate (`refreshOf`). Uncapped, a frame waits for the GPU to be done with all but the last frame sent (`AHEAD`, a fence in each frame's commands: `glFence`, `gpuFence`, set by `renderer.ts`): begun as soon as the last frame's commands were sent, a GPU-bound game queued 4 or 5 frames ahead of the GPU and a press waited behind them all (from play: "Vsync off raises FPS but makes the game feel laggy"): 152 ms from the key to the GPU done with the frame that read it, against 34 synced; now 22 (`npm run stalls -- --scenario latency --url "this?vsync=1,this?vsync=0" --window 2560x1440 --scale 2`). On WebGPU 29 ms without the fence, 16 with it (`onSubmittedWorkDone`). Where the CPU is the slower side (`--cpu-slowdown 4`) no frame waits and nothing is lost: none of some 600 frames a round waited, the lag and frames a second a fence-less loop's (`?ahead=99`) within the noise. - `framePace.ts`: the game's step a frame is the frames' mean interval, not each one's own (`pacedStep`, from `frame` in `main.ts`; Auto and the perf recorder read the raw). A browser can time its frames on another screen's ticks than the one the window is on: Chrome on Windows on a 60 Hz monitor beside a 145 Hz one gave its frames 2 and 3 of the 145 Hz ticks in turn, 13.8 and 20.7 ms, while the screen showed each for 16.7 (traces T9NG-CZ79, GYKX-N2GP, 0HSJ-60QV), and stepped by those the world went a half further in one shown frame than the next: a judder at an even 60 (#552, "playing on a secondary monitor with lower refresh rate lags"). What the frames' clock has and the steps haven't is paid back a tenth a frame, so game time stays within a frame of it; a frame 1.5 times off the mean (a hitch, a refresh missed) pays all that's owed at once, and a run of them (the window moved to a screen of another rate) takes the mean to the new rate. Three frames of the very same interval step by exactly it from then on, so the lab's even clock steps as it did. Uncapped frames are stepped as they come. `npm run lab -- pace` (`--canary`: stepped as they come, through `window.__holdFramePace`, a dev build's alone), the pacer's own tests in `lab:test` (`framePace.test.mjs`). The detector's display rate (`refreshOf` in `perf/detect.ts`) is still the fastest second of the session, so after a move from 144 Hz to 60 it judges against 144's frame; that sends no report on its own (a steady 60 isn't under the mean of 50) and changes nothing the player sees.

## Gotchas

- **Bright effects:** light adds up, so the final grade (`core/renderer.ts`) compresses it the way the eye does, and effects and their lights stay at full strength: don't dim single effects to fix a bright scene.
  - **Adaptation:** after bloom, the average light over a 16x9 and a 4x3 grid of the frame (the engine's `fs_area` in Bulwark's `post.wgsl`, after WebGL's `AreaPass`) is held under per-view targets (`ADAPT`, just above each view's ordinary scenes, which it leaves untouched) by compressing only the light above `ADAPT_BASE`, on a power curve. Never scale a whole pixel by its area's brightness (the dim ground round an effect darkens into a shadow around it), and don't adapt before bloom (effects drop under bloom's threshold and turn into flat discs with no glow). Above `HI_KNEE` the brightest channel eases towards `HI_CAP`, and in a large glare tone mapping keeps a bright pixel's colour (`HUE_*`, `GLARE_*`), so a big bright area stays green or blue instead of turning white.
  - **Distance:** effect materials dim with their distance from the camera (`nearGlow` in `core/materials.ts`: full strength from the top-down camera's distance, down to a quarter up close). `additive()`, the glow particles, projectile cores (a hostile bolt's is an orb of added light, with a pooled light when one is free; the spore throwers' are `fx/sporeOrb.ts`, the Rift Witch's and the Colossus's `fx/riftBolts.ts`: blended orbs, with added-light tails and halos), entity glows (`kit.glow`, the staff orb included; a hero's gear glows opt out) and the hit flash on bodies have it; wrap a new effect material in `nearGlow()`. The world's own glows don't.
  - **First person:** particles fade within 3.5m of the camera, a bolt flying at the camera shrinks away over its last metres (never pops out: nothing near the camera may switch off at once), and a cast leaves from `Player.castPoint` / `palmPoint`, which in first person are the weapon and hand in view. Keep lights off the camera: above the head, not at 2m. An effect round the body (a heal, a war cry) is seen from inside it: `Player.eyes` tells a skill it is, so it can put its particles where they're seen (a wider ring) and its light ahead of the view (`Player.lightAhead`; straight above, the weapons' steel mirrors it back into the view). The highlights on the hero's own materials are eased there from half of `SHINE.eyes` towards it (`core/materials.ts`, `kit.u.uShine`, set each frame by his player, 0 in the other views): a hand's breadth from the eyes, the sun on a blade's flat blew it out white and bloomed over 7.7% of the frame, its rougher grime left as dark blots across it. `lab haze` measures it.
  - Nothing large may flash or pulse faster than about 1.5 times a second: 3+ flashes a second is a photosensitivity hazard. A hit's flash on a body is full at most every 0.7s (`hitFlash` in `util.ts`). A pattern moving across the view flashes too: Maelstrom's wide ribbons passing a point 4 times a second, and its lashes of lightning at every damage tick, each flashed a sixteenth of the screen 3 to 4 times a second.
  - **Measure an effect** with `npm run lab -- cast <skill>` (`--class`, against main: commit, swap the skill's files in from `origin/main`, run, swap back): every frame of it drawn and read back in each biome and view, with the biome's foes coming at the hero. Its light is held to the budget from the owner's screenshots (it raises the frame's mean by 50 at most, its brightest sixteenth 150, 5% of the frame bright, as stored), its flashes are counted as WCAG counts them (a sixteenth of the screen each), and the shaders compiled after loading must be none. Read its timeline before changing anything: a mean that leapt as Maelstrom's first tick landed in first person was a dark foe in front of the camera dying, and a bright rock behind it showing.
- **The low-Health look** (`core/lowLife.ts`, the grade in `core/renderer.ts`): under half of his most Health the local hero's frame drains towards grey and blood seeps in from the screen's edges, both growing as the Health falls (the drain to 90% at none), with a heartbeat of 0.9 to 1.4 a second (none with reduced motion). It's all uniforms of the final grade (`setLowLife`), no shader change. The blood is vector shapes painted once on a canvas for the screen's shape (`core/bloodEdges.ts`: a seep with a wavy front in from every edge, deepest in the corners, drips from the top, droplets and clots, from one seed), into a mask whose green is how near to nothing the Health must be for each pixel to show, so the grade shows what the Health reaches; nothing reaches further in than 0.29 of the screen's height (`MOST_IN`). Painted again only once a new size has held for 250 ms. Page elements over the canvas would repaint every frame (see the overlays' gotcha). Measure with `npm run lab -- health` (`--canary`): at none, 65 to 83% of the colour drained and blood on 19 to 26% of the screen, none in its middle.
- **Canvas size:** `fitViewport` in `core/renderer.ts` fits the canvas, render targets and camera on resize *and* every frame, and ignores zero sizes. Moving the window between monitors can report a zero height mid-move (zero-sized render targets: a black screen until the next resize) or change the pixel ratio with no resize event. Read the window size through it, not `innerWidth`/`innerHeight` directly, in anything that feeds the camera. The canvas is the screen's own pixels, and the scene and its post chain render the quality's share of them (`sceneRatio`, `sceneSize`), the last pass scaling it up: anything sized in the scene's pixels (the aim laser's width) reads those, not the canvas's. Check a screen with the lab's `--screen` (`1280x720@3`: a 4K screen at 300%), which puts the page on it as a move between monitors does, and two with `--screens`.
- **HDR output** (`core/hdr.ts`, the pause menu's HDR: Auto or Off; **not ported to the engine**, whose frame is SDR, `src/engine/AGENTS.md`: what follows is how three's renderers did it): on a display in HDR mode (`dynamic-range: high`) the frame's highlights go past the screen's white, and everything under them shows as on an SDR screen.
  - **The way out:** a WebGL canvas can't be shown in extended range (Chrome 149 has no tone mapping mode for it), so its drawing buffer is made float16 (`drawingBufferStorage`, with `EXT_color_buffer_float` on, or it stays 8-bit) and each frame is copied onto a WebGPU canvas configured `toneMapping: { mode: 'extended' }` (`copyExternalImageToTexture`, on the GPU, at the end of `render()`). The copy takes the colour as it is (`premultipliedAlpha: true`: by default the copy divided the colour by the frame's alpha). The grade writes that alpha as 1, on WebGPU too (`inHdr`): passed on from the scene it was 1 and one more for each layer of added light, 3 through the forest's shafts of light, which Windows showed the same and a Mac's screen multiplied the colour by, flat bright bands where the shafts stood (#575, from a photo of a MacBook; `lab light --hdr` reads the shown canvas's alpha). The WebGL canvas stays under the HDR one, invisible, and still takes the pointer. Both canvases hold extended sRGB, encoded (0.5 shows as 127, past 1 brighter than white). On WebGPU (`webgpu.ts`) the renderer's own canvas is made in extended range as it starts (`initHdrForWebGPU`), and the grade's last node does what the uniforms below do; three configures its canvas once, so a switch shows after a reload there.
  - **The grade** does the rest in uniforms, never a shader change: under `HDR_KNEE` (half of white) the frame is the SDR one as a gamma 2.2 screen shows it (Windows shows SDR content by the sRGB curve, which lifts its shadows), above it the highlights grow by their brightest channel towards the peak (hue kept), all of it at the paper white, and the highlights' cap grows with the room the peak gives over the paper white (`uCap`), or every effect's core would stay at SDR's.
  - **The levels** are shares of the OS's white for SDR content, as no page can read a screen's nits: Brightness (the paper white: lower it if the daylit forest is too bright) and Highlights (the peak), shown only while HDR is on and kept in `last-stand-hdr`. `?hdr=1` puts it on with any display (on an SDR one, past white is clipped), `?hdr=0` never.
  - **Measure** with `npm run lab -- light --hdr` (every pixel under the knee against the SDR frame at the paper white, nothing past the peak, the canvas shown read back against the frame drawn, with `--canary`) and the SDR frames with `light --ab`, which agree with main line for line. What it costs (a float16 drawing buffer and a copy of the frame a frame) hasn't been measured on a phone, where HDR displays are common.

- **A point light lights only where it reaches** (`POINT_LIGHTS_LOOPED` in `renderer.ts`): three works out every point light's lighting at every lit pixel, its colour 0 or not, so each pixel paid for all 20 in view (the biome's 10, the pool's 8 sitting at intensity 0, the hero's own two), each past its `distance` too. The lighting (and a casting light's shadow lookup) is skipped where its colour came out 0 (three's own `directLight.visible`), which changes no pixel (`lab light --ab`: all 18 pictures the same): in the forest at 4.4 Mpx on Low's settings the point lights fell from 2.29 ms of a 3.9 ms scene pass to 0.96 (`lab scenecost --ab`, the owner's 4070 Ti). On WebGPU the same test passes over them in three's `DynamicLighting` loop (`loopPassesUnreached` in `webgpu.ts`, three's `PointLightDataNode` setup with it): it worked out the physical model's whole direct term for every light at every pixel, and at a phone's pixels the forest's point lights took 13.8 ms of the scene's 18.2, 5.0 after (`lab passes --features --device phone --level verylow`; `--ab=HEAD`'s pictures the same); on the owner's phone the lit shading was 61.5 ms of a 78.7 ms frame (trace V08X-13SR). Give every point light a `distance`: one with none (0) lights every pixel. What's left of their cost is the loop over them, not their lighting: at a 4K laptop's 8.3 Mpx on Low (trace D5RK-S3DD: 16 fps, the GPU 57 ms a frame) the forest's point lights read 1.4 to 1.8 ms of a 4.3 ms scene pass, and their lighting where they reach 0.08 (`lab scenecost`'s "where they reach": the same programs, the lights put out of reach). Timed at a phone's 4.5 Mpx, where that GPU idles, it read as nothing (±0.25): time a change of this kind loaded. What was tried and is out: a loop over a list of the lit lights alone, its bound a uniform and each light read through the list (the forest's pass 3.8 ms to 6.5: a loop the compiler can't unroll, over a struct array indexed by a value it can't know, costs more a light than the constant loop it replaced, though it visited half as many), and the opaque draws sorted by program before material (within the noise), and a grid of the lights reaching each cell of the floor, a bit a light in a texture read once a pixel, the loop passing a light over on its bit (7% under the same shader with every bit set, but nothing under main: the read and the bits cost what they saved over the packed test, the crypt 5.84 ms against main's 5.68). **A read that takes the screen's gradients can't be left out by a branch**: a plain `texture()` under an `if` is worked out at every pixel and then picked (D3D's compiler flattens the branch, as the gradients must exist on both sides), so the two casting lights' shadows, five taps each (three's soft edges), were read at every lit pixel, in the light's reach or not: 1.3 ms of the crypt's 5.7 ms scene pass and 2.6 of the forest's 12.6. Their taps are read at the map's one level (`textureGrad` with no gradients, `pointShadowInReach` in `renderer.ts`; a shadow map has no other level, so the pixels are the same: `lab light --ab` at High and at Low, all 18), and the branch then leaves them out: the scene pass 14% shorter in the crypt and 17% in the forest, at every level. An `if`/`else` round a plain `texture()` saved nothing at all, and an early `return` before it made the five taps 0.25 ms slower: check a branch round a texture read by its row in `lab scenecost`, never by reading the shader. **The still shadows keep their five soft taps at every level**: read once on Low and Very low (no radius: 6 to 7% of the scene pass more) the fires were blinding in some frames (from play, on #490's preview; `lab fire` and `lab light` read nothing of it, 0.02 to 0.16% of a frame's pixels changed). WebGPU's taps are its nodes'. A shorter reach on Low saved 0.02 to 0.05 ms more, and the still shadows left unread 0.04 to 0.19, with the brazier lighting its own plinth through its bowl: neither was kept.
