# `src/core`

- `inputDelivery.ts` aggregates key arrival/handler/editing counts, pointer presses, pointer movements and maximum DOM delivery delays once a second during manual recording. Movement without clicks must be counted: windowed trace 65SQ-60GS showed steady frames while the player reported a roughly 5 FPS cursor for ten seconds, without clicking or testing keys. Capture is installed before UI key interceptors. A zero-arrival second with live frames differs from captured keys swallowed before the game's handler. No key codes, coordinates, text or event objects are retained. DOM timestamps cannot measure time spent in a native queue before the browser creates the event.

- `keyDelivery.ts` measures DOM dispatch delay from a valid monotonic timestamp, not the key's value. Ignore missing, future, epoch and non-finite stamps. `main.ts` records at most one delayed-delivery mark a second, plus focus tags; no key codes or typed text enter traces. It cannot alone detect a native queue that delays the event before that timestamp exists; compare manual arrival counts as well.

The engine under the game.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `renderer.ts`: the renderer, its post chain (the scene in an MSAA target of its own; bloom, the area's light and the grade on single-sample targets, each reading the scene's target itself, its NaN and Inf pixels cleaned and what he can't see fogged as it reads it, `SCENE_READ`, and bloom added by the grade and the area: a pass of its own cleaned it into a target of the frame's size and bloom was blended over that in another, two passes over every pixel, from play an S25 Edge's post chain 9.4 ms of its 33 ms frame; `lab light --ab` against the commit before agrees in the close views but for the film grain's hash, a few levels in its noise, as a shader compiled otherwise rounds it otherwise), the canvas's fit, the final grade and the camera rig in each view.

Each is painted on its canvases as it comes (a map asked for before is handed out blank, and a warm-up waits for the maps its materials draw with, `mapsArrived`); a production build keeps them across visits in IndexedDB by the worker script's name (`kept.ts`), so a return reads them back in a few ms. - `frameLoop.ts`: the game's frames, each as the browser asks for one (vsync, the default) or uncapped (the pause menu's Vsync: Off, the cookie `last-stand-vsync`, `?vsync=0` for a visit), each drawn as soon as the last is done, through a `MessageChannel` (a nested timeout is held to 4 ms); a hidden tab always waits for the browser. Uncapped is for measuring: the screen shows its own rate either way, but synced to a 60 Hz screen the GPU's time a frame read 14 ms where it was 7, waiting for the screen to take the frame, and a faster build looked like a slower one once both reached the screen's rate. The owner's lobby (stalls, `this,this?vsync=0`, the overlay read): synced 137 to 142 fps, the GPU 5.57 ms; uncapped 178 to 182, the GPU 3.51 ms, the CPU's 5.5 to 6 ms holding it. The perf report and the traces say `vsync off`, and its frames never set the display's rate (`refreshOf`). Uncapped, a frame waits for the GPU to be done with all but the last frame sent (`AHEAD`, a fence in each frame's commands: `glFence`, `gpuFence`, set by `renderer.ts`): begun as soon as the last frame's commands were sent, a GPU-bound game queued 4 or 5 frames ahead of the GPU and a press waited behind them all (from play: "Vsync off raises FPS but makes the game feel laggy"): 152 ms from the key to the GPU done with the frame that read it, against 34 synced; now 22 (`npm run stalls -- --scenario latency --url "this?vsync=1,this?vsync=0" --window 2560x1440 --scale 2`). On WebGPU 29 ms without the fence, 16 with it (`onSubmittedWorkDone`; WebGPURenderer's WebGL 2 backend, `?renderer=webgpu-gl`, takes the GL fence). Where the CPU is the slower side (`--cpu-slowdown 4`) no frame waits and nothing is lost: none of some 600 frames a round waited, the lag and frames a second a fence-less loop's (`?ahead=99`) within the noise. Compare such settings on one build in turns (`?ahead=N`, `?frameWaits` counting the frames that waited): two builds compared so read the fence 20 ms and 10% of the frames worse, which the waits, none, showed was noise between the builds. `holdFrames(true)` holds the game's steps while the loop keeps asking for frames: the profiler (`src/perf/profiler.ts`) draws its own frames meanwhile. `renderer.ts` `turnBy` turns the camera round what it follows in any view (watching a co-op run).
- `framePace.ts`: the game's step a frame is the frames' mean interval, not each one's own (`pacedStep`, from `frame` in `main.ts`; Auto and the perf recorder read the raw). A browser can time its frames on another screen's ticks than the one the window is on: Chrome on Windows on a 60 Hz monitor beside a 145 Hz one gave its frames 2 and 3 of the 145 Hz ticks in turn, 13.8 and 20.7 ms, while the screen showed each for 16.7 (traces T9NG-CZ79, GYKX-N2GP, 0HSJ-60QV), and stepped by those the world went a half further in one shown frame than the next: a judder at an even 60 (#552, "playing on a secondary monitor with lower refresh rate lags"). What the frames' clock has and the steps haven't is paid back a tenth a frame, so game time stays within a frame of it; a frame 1.5 times off the mean (a hitch, a refresh missed) pays all that's owed at once, and a run of them (the window moved to a screen of another rate) takes the mean to the new rate. Three frames of the very same interval step by exactly it from then on, so the lab's even clock steps as it did. Uncapped frames are stepped as they come. `npm run lab -- pace` (`--canary`: stepped as they come, through `window.__holdFramePace`, a dev build's alone), the pacer's own tests in `lab:test` (`framePace.test.mjs`). The detector's display rate (`refreshOf` in `perf/detect.ts`) is still the fastest second of the session, so after a move from 144 Hz to 60 it judges against 144's frame; that sends no report on its own (a steady 60 isn't under the mean of 50) and changes nothing the player sees.
- `timers.ts`: the game-time scheduler (it respects pause, unlike `setTimeout`). `cookies.ts`: small preferences.
- `tuning.ts`: values a dev build changes while it runs (`tunable(name, values)`; the lab's `--tune name.field=...` sets them before a command): a pose or a camera compared across values without editing and booting again. ## Gotchas

- **Bright effects:** light adds up, so the final grade (`core/renderer.ts`) compresses it the way the eye does, and effects and their lights stay at full strength: don't dim single effects to fix a bright scene.
  - **Adaptation:** after bloom, the average light over a 16x9 and a 4x3 grid of the frame (`AreaPass`) is held under per-view targets (`ADAPT`, just above each view's ordinary scenes, which it leaves untouched) by compressing only the light above `ADAPT_BASE`, on a power curve. Never scale a whole pixel by its area's brightness (the dim ground round an effect darkens into a shadow around it), and don't adapt before bloom (effects drop under bloom's threshold and turn into flat discs with no glow). Above `HI_KNEE` the brightest channel eases towards `HI_CAP`, and in a large glare tone mapping keeps a bright pixel's colour (`HUE_*`, `GLARE_*`), so a big bright area stays green or blue instead of turning white.
  - **Distance:** effect materials dim with their distance from the camera (`nearGlow` in `core/materials.ts`: full strength from the top-down camera's distance, down to a quarter up close). `additive()`, the glow particles, projectile cores (a hostile bolt's is an orb of added light, with a pooled light when one is free; the spore throwers' are `fx/sporeOrb.ts`, the Rift Witch's and the Colossus's `fx/riftBolts.ts`: blended orbs, with added-light tails and halos), entity glows (`kit.glow`, the staff orb included; a hero's gear glows opt out) and the hit flash on bodies have it; wrap a new effect material in `nearGlow()`. The world's own glows don't.
  - **First person:** particles fade within 3.5m of the camera, a bolt flying at the camera shrinks away over its last metres (never pops out: nothing near the camera may switch off at once), and a cast leaves from `Player.castPoint` / `palmPoint`, which in first person are the weapon and hand in view. Keep lights off the camera: above the head, not at 2m. An effect round the body (a heal, a war cry) is seen from inside it: `Player.eyes` tells a skill it is, so it can put its particles where they're seen (a wider ring) and its light ahead of the view (`Player.lightAhead`; straight above, the weapons' steel mirrors it back into the view). The highlights on the hero's own materials are eased there from half of `SHINE.eyes` towards it (`core/materials.ts`, `kit.u.uShine`, set each frame by his player, 0 in the other views): a hand's breadth from the eyes, the sun on a blade's flat blew it out white and bloomed over 7.7% of the frame, its rougher grime left as dark blots across it. `lab haze` measures it.
  - Nothing large may flash or pulse faster than about 1.5 times a second: 3+ flashes a second is a photosensitivity hazard. A hit's flash on a body is full at most every 0.7s (`hitFlash` in `util.ts`). A pattern moving across the view flashes too: Maelstrom's wide ribbons passing a point 4 times a second, and its lashes of lightning at every damage tick, each flashed a sixteenth of the screen 3 to 4 times a second.
  - **Measure an effect** with `npm run lab -- cast <skill>` (`--class`, against main: commit, swap the skill's files in from `origin/main`, run, swap back): every frame of it drawn and read back in each biome and view, with the biome's foes coming at the hero. Its light is held to the budget from the owner's screenshots (it raises the frame's mean by 50 at most, its brightest sixteenth 150, 5% of the frame bright, as stored), its flashes are counted as WCAG counts them (a sixteenth of the screen each), and the shaders compiled after loading must be none. Read its timeline before changing anything: a mean that leapt as Maelstrom's first tick landed in first person was a dark foe in front of the camera dying, and a bright rock behind it showing.
- **The low-Health look** (`core/lowLife.ts`, the grade in `core/renderer.ts`): under half of his most Health the local hero's frame drains towards grey and blood seeps in from the screen's edges, both growing as the Health falls (the drain to 90% at none), with a heartbeat of 0.9 to 1.4 a second (none with reduced motion). It's all uniforms of the final grade (`setLowLife`), no shader change. The blood is vector shapes painted once on a canvas for the screen's shape (`core/bloodEdges.ts`: a seep with a wavy front in from every edge, deepest in the corners, drips from the top, droplets and clots, from one seed), into a mask whose green is how near to nothing the Health must be for each pixel to show, so the grade shows what the Health reaches; nothing reaches further in than 0.29 of the screen's height (`MOST_IN`). Painted again only once a new size has held for 250 ms. Page elements over the canvas would repaint every frame (see the overlays' gotcha). Measure with `npm run lab -- health` (`--canary`): at none, 65 to 83% of the colour drained and blood on 19 to 26% of the screen, none in its middle.
- **Canvas size:** `fitViewport` in `core/renderer.ts` fits the canvas, render targets and camera on resize *and* every frame, and ignores zero sizes. Moving the window between monitors can report a zero height mid-move (zero-sized render targets: a black screen until the next resize) or change the pixel ratio with no resize event. Read the window size through it, not `innerWidth`/`innerHeight` directly, in anything that feeds the camera. The canvas is the screen's own pixels, and the scene and its post chain render the quality's share of them (`sceneRatio`, `sceneSize`), the last pass scaling it up: anything sized in the scene's pixels (the aim laser's width) reads those, not the canvas's. Check a screen with the lab's `--screen` (`1280x720@3`: a 4K screen at 300%), which puts the page on it as a move between monitors does, and two with `--screens`.
- **HDR output** (`core/hdr.ts`, the pause menu's HDR: Auto or Off): on a display in HDR mode (`dynamic-range: high`) the frame's highlights go past the screen's white, and everything under them shows as on an SDR screen.
  - **The way out:** a WebGL canvas can't be shown in extended range (Chrome 149 has no tone mapping mode for it), so its drawing buffer is made float16 (`drawingBufferStorage`, with `EXT_color_buffer_float` on, or it stays 8-bit) and each frame is copied onto a WebGPU canvas configured `toneMapping: { mode: 'extended' }` (`copyExternalImageToTexture`, on the GPU, at the end of `render()`). The copy takes the colour as it is (`premultipliedAlpha: true`: by default the copy divided the colour by the frame's alpha). The grade writes that alpha as 1, on WebGPU too (`inHdr`): passed on from the scene it was 1 and one more for each layer of added light, 3 through the forest's shafts of light, which Windows showed the same and a Mac's screen multiplied the colour by, flat bright bands where the shafts stood (#575, from a photo of a MacBook; `lab light --hdr` reads the shown canvas's alpha). The WebGL canvas stays under the HDR one, invisible, and still takes the pointer. Both canvases hold extended sRGB, encoded (0.5 shows as 127, past 1 brighter than white). On WebGPU (`webgpu.ts`) the renderer's own canvas is made in extended range as it starts (`initHdrForWebGPU`), and the grade's last node does what the uniforms below do; three configures its canvas once, so a switch shows after a reload there.
  - **The grade** does the rest in uniforms, never a shader change: under `HDR_KNEE` (half of white) the frame is the SDR one as a gamma 2.2 screen shows it (Windows shows SDR content by the sRGB curve, which lifts its shadows), above it the highlights grow by their brightest channel towards the peak (hue kept), all of it at the paper white, and the highlights' cap grows with the room the peak gives over the paper white (`uCap`), or every effect's core would stay at SDR's.
  - **The levels** are shares of the OS's white for SDR content, as no page can read a screen's nits: Brightness (the paper white: lower it if the daylit forest is too bright) and Highlights (the peak), shown only while HDR is on and kept in `last-stand-hdr`. `?hdr=1` puts it on with any display (on an SDR one, past white is clipped), `?hdr=0` never.
  - **Measure** with `npm run lab -- light --hdr` (every pixel under the knee against the SDR frame at the paper white, nothing past the peak, the canvas shown read back against the frame drawn, with `--canary`) and the SDR frames with `light --ab`, which agree with main line for line. What it costs (a float16 drawing buffer and a copy of the frame a frame) hasn't been measured on a phone, where HDR displays are common.
- **The environment's reflection is shadowed** (`shadeEnvironment` in `renderer.ts`, three's lit shaders' chunks patched as the renderer starts): three lights a surface's reflection of the environment whatever stands between it and the sky, and each biome's environment has a bright panel where its sun or moon stands, so the crypt's glossy marble walks shone as bright in a column's shadow as out of it. The sun's shadow on a point now leaves a quarter of that reflection (`ENV_IN_SHADOW`) and half of the ambient light (`AMBIENT_IN_SHADOW`: the marble is lit mostly by its ambient, and with the reflection alone shadowed a box's shadow on a walk still hardly showed). The shadows' edges are soft by a fixed width on the ground (`PENUMBRA` in `quality.ts`: the shadow map's radius scaled by its size, so each quality level blurs as wide). Through the eyes a tree's shadow across a walk now shows (`lab light --ab`: only shaded pixels change; the frames' means fall by 0.010 at most, under the forest's canopy, and by 0.001 in the crypt). A three that changes the chunks logs an error and leaves the reflection unshadowed.
- **Quality presets** (`data/quality.ts`, pause menu, Auto by default) change the scene's pixel ratio, MSAA, shadow map size, which small character parts cast shadows, the floors' relief (its steps a uniform) and how far apart the village's grass stands (`grassSpread`: its cells, tiles and strands uniforms, a geometry swapped) and the lamps' reach and shadows (`lampReach`, `lampShadows`: the list's reaches and its shadows' flag, `setLampQuality` in `lamps.ts`) and whether the village's kept shadow maps are drawn over only as what moves moves (`shadowsOnMove`, `world/shadowsOnMove.ts`) and the share of the scene's pixels bloom draws at (`bloomShare`, `setBloomShare` in `renderer.ts`: its targets sized again and its blurs' steps, uniforms; Low's half each way, from play an S25 Edge's bloom 6 ms of an 80 ms frame: its passes draw a quarter of the pixels, each level as wide on the screen as the next one up was, and by night in the village it adds under half a thousandth of white to the frame's mean either way, `lab village --only bloomOnLow`; WebGL's alone, WebGPU's bloom is its own) and the rounds a skirt's cloth is draped over the limbs a frame (`drapeRounds`, `setDrapeRounds` in `entities/models/armor.ts`: the drape a third of the hero's update, Low's 2 against 4, its last pass off the limbs kept), all live and without shader changes. **Very low** is under Low, for a device that still lags there (from play: "a very low graphics mode, even lower than low... all graphics reduced, effects that use less GPU processing"): a 512 shadow map, only parts 0.3 m across or more casting, the sun's shadow drawn every other frame at any frame rate (`shadowInTurns`, `setShadowInTurns` in `renderer.ts`, WebGL too), the heroes at a fifth of their detail, leg IK within 6 m, no bloom at all (`bloomShare` 0: WebGL leaves its pass out, WebGPU adds its glow at no strength; at a quarter of the scene's pixels each way its glow flickered as the view moved, and from play it still did once its bright pass read every pixel: "maybe remove the bloom ?"), a burst's, a puff's and debris' particles halved (`particles`, `setParticleShare` in `fx/particles.ts`), the sun's shadow of what stands still alone, drawn only when that changes (`stillSunShadow`, `setSunShadowStill` in `world/props.ts`: the heroes and foes cast none by it), a third of the floor's clutter (`clutter`, `setClutterShare` in `world/culling.ts`), half of each thicket bush's leaf cards (`leaves`, `setLeafShare` in `world/culling.ts`: a draw range, each bush's cards merged a lump's each in turn by `canopyGeo`, so any share covers every lump alike; at a phone's pixels the thicket's leaves were a quarter of the forest's scene pass, 41.4 ms of about 145 on an AMD integrated GPU, and 24.1 halved, the scene about 118: `lab scenecost --biomes forest --each --level verylow --screen 832x384@3.75 --boot verylow --ab`; the bushes' cores show between the cards), and the village's grass spread four times, a sixteenth of High's blades (`grassSpread` 4, `docs/agents/village-grass.md`); the screen's pixels kept, as at every level. **Very low is held to its triangles** (from play, a phone's report at 577k: "why are we rendering 600k tris on very low on phone", "we should try to render 100k tris first"): count them with `npm run lab -- draws --biome forest --count 0 --boot verylow` (and `--boot low` for the sun's shadow pass, which Very low no longer draws a frame). The forest's lobby was 483k on a frame that drew the shadow (the scene 266k, the shadow pass 216k), the crypt's 386k; they are 230k and 199k, every frame. What's left, largest first: the forest's thicket 28k, its four stumps 26k, the crypt's small dead trees and roots 27k, its three columns 25k, the trees' bark and leaves, and the hero 22 to 24k (from 41 to 60k, #536, `lab turnaround --boot verylow --parts` by part: from play, "wayy too much detail, especially for the hands"). A hero booted at Very low has his hands meshed on a 5.6 mm grid and simplified by meshoptimizer to about 500 triangles a side, his nails a plate of four quads (`setHandsCoarse` in `models/handShape.ts`: a hand was 12,500 triangles, then 4,800 with #514's grid alone); below Low's detail each piece's least (`lod`'s `min` in `models/armor.ts`) comes down with the detail, and the gear's belts, plates, rims, studs, eyes and neck, which kept their count at every level, come down too (`lod(n, n)`); the stitched seams are left off (the ranger's 11.9k and the mage's 9.8k: a millimetre's thread). High to Low build the same as before. **A level cuts the same on both renderers** (from play: "cut same effects on same quality level, on webgl and webgpu, in a similar way, so they look roughly similar"): a cut is a count, an `autoUpdate` or a uniform both draw by. Auto steps down to it from Low, and far below its target goes there at once. On WebGPU the sun's shadow map takes its size once, as it's first made (`apply` in `quality.ts`): three resizes it from `mapSize`, but the lit materials kept the old map's binding, and every frame's submit was refused (`Destroyed texture ShadowDepthTexture used in a submit`), the scene's picture standing still as the game went on; a phone's hero stood stuck mid pose, the stick moving nothing, once Auto stepped down a level (trace 4CP9-45EC). `lab profile --renderer webgpu --level medium` holds it (main: 0% of the screen's middle changed as the hero walked). Keep new settings in that category, or they cause compile hitches when switched. The scene's pixel ratio is often less than the canvas's (Low on a phone: a 3120x1440 canvas, the scene 1048x483), so anything sized in pixels sizes itself by the scene's target (`pointScale()` for points), not the canvas: the particles were 3 times too wide there.
- **High is tuned for the GPU, not cut** (`lab scenecost --level high --views first --screen 2560x1440@1 --ab`, an RTX 3060): its sun's shadow map 3072, not 4096 (the sun's shadow in the forest's scene pass 3.98 ms to 3.00, and its own pass 44% fewer texels), no caster under 0.09 m (from 0.07), the floors' relief in 12 steps (from 16: the lit shading without its maps 2.77 ms to 1.88) and the skirts draped 3 rounds a frame (from 4); the forest's first-person scene pass 19.4 ms to 18.2. Pictured against main (`lab light --views first --boot high --ab`): a few % of the pixels differ, along shadows' edges and the floors' relief, the frame as bright. What's left of High's cost is its content: in the forest's first-person scene pass the cut-out foliage 13 ms, the point lights 8, the lamps' and sun's shadows 4 each, the environment 2.3; and HDR's output, where shown, the most of all.
- **HDR is High's alone** (`QualityPreset.hdr`, `setHdrAllowed` in `hdr.ts`; the pause menu's HDR says "(off below High)" where the level keeps it off): its way out, a float16 drawing buffer copied onto the HDR canvas every frame at the screen's own pixels, was 26 ms of a 38 ms frame on a laptop's integrated GPU with a 4K panel, the scene 4.6 (trace BN6P-FN12), and 7.1 to 9.0 ms of 12 on the owner's 4070 Ti at the same screen (`lab scenecost --frame --screen 1280x720@3`). So Auto's first step down, to Medium, lets it go. WebGPU makes its canvas in HDR or not once, as the renderer is made, before the level is put to use: it's told the level the boot starts at (`hdrAllowedAtBoot` in `quality.ts`, `initHdrForWebGPU`), as made in HDR whatever the level a phone's Very low drew a float16 canvas at 3120x1440 (trace GSB0-GKVW), where WebGL drew none. The copy is far slower than its bytes: making it cheap is its own work.
- **MSAA follows the screen's density** (`samplesOn` in `renderer.ts`): a level's samples divided by the screen's pixels per css px, to 4, 2 or none (High's and Medium's 2 none from a ratio of 2; a 4 would be 2 at 2 and none at 3), as a dense screen's pixels are already as fine as the samples would make an edge, and its samples cost the most (a 4K laptop at 300% drew 6.1 Mpx with 4 samples a pixel, its frame 119 ms, trace 11CW-PY3R). Fitted again when the window moves to a screen of another density; the perf report and the traces say the samples used. WebGPU's are chosen once.
- **A point light lights only where it reaches** (`POINT_LIGHTS_LOOPED` in `renderer.ts`): three works out every point light's lighting at every lit pixel, its colour 0 or not, so each pixel paid for all 20 in view (the biome's 10, the pool's 8 sitting at intensity 0, the hero's own two), each past its `distance` too. The lighting (and a casting light's shadow lookup) is skipped where its colour came out 0 (three's own `directLight.visible`), which changes no pixel (`lab light --ab`: all 18 pictures the same): in the forest at 4.4 Mpx on Low's settings the point lights fell from 2.29 ms of a 3.9 ms scene pass to 0.96 (`lab scenecost --ab`, the owner's 4070 Ti). A laptop's integrated GPU spent 86% of its frame on that pass. On WebGPU the same test passes over them in three's `DynamicLighting` loop (`loopPassesUnreached` in `webgpu.ts`, three's `PointLightDataNode` setup with it): it worked out the physical model's whole direct term for every light at every pixel, and at a phone's pixels the forest's point lights took 13.8 ms of the scene's 18.2, 5.0 after (`lab passes --features --device phone --level verylow`; `--ab=HEAD`'s pictures the same); on the owner's phone the lit shading was 61.5 ms of a 78.7 ms frame (trace V08X-13SR). Give every point light a `distance`: one with none (0) lights every pixel. What's left of their cost is the loop over them, not their lighting: at a 4K laptop's 8.3 Mpx on Low (trace D5RK-S3DD: 16 fps, the GPU 57 ms a frame) the forest's point lights read 1.4 to 1.8 ms of a 4.3 ms scene pass, and their lighting where they reach 0.08 (`lab scenecost`'s "where they reach": the same programs, the lights put out of reach). So an idle light, or a point past a light's reach, is passed over in the unshadowed loop on a test of the distance's square, before three works out its direction, distance and attenuation (`toLight`; the same pixels: `lab light --ab` and `--boot low`, all 18 the same): the scene pass about 15% shorter in the forest and 9% in the crypt (`lab scenecost --ab --fresh`, 4.25 to 3.6 ms and 2.15 to 1.95, on a GPU another session was using). A light the loop passes over is tested on one packed value, read before the light itself (`lightBounds.ts`: each light's place as the camera sees it and its reach's square, 0 for one that lights nothing, in a list every lit material shares, packed anew at each render for its camera; the loop's bound stays a constant): 13% of the scene pass with the GPU loaded, the crypt's 6.49 ms to 5.64 and the forest's 13.78 to 11.98 at 20.7 Mpx on a 4080 laptop (`lab scenecost --screen 2880x1800@2`, ±0.03), the pixels the same (`lab light --ab`). Timed at a phone's 4.5 Mpx, where that GPU idles, it read as nothing (±0.25): time a change of this kind loaded. What was tried and is out: a loop over a list of the lit lights alone, its bound a uniform and each light read through the list (the forest's pass 3.8 ms to 6.5: a loop the compiler can't unroll, over a struct array indexed by a value it can't know, costs more a light than the constant loop it replaced, though it visited half as many), and the opaque draws sorted by program before material (within the noise). A shorter reach on Low saved 0.02 to 0.05 ms more, and the still shadows left unread 0.04 to 0.19, with the brazier lighting its own plinth through its bowl: neither was kept.
- **Bloom on Low draws at half the scene's pixels each way** (`bloomShare`, `setBloomShare` in `renderer.ts`: its targets sized again and its blurs' steps, uniforms): its bright pass, five levels' blurs and composite draw a quarter of the pixels, each level as wide on the screen as the next one up was (from play, an S25 Edge's bloom 6 ms of an 80 ms frame at full res; by night it adds under half a thousandth of white to the frame's mean either way). On WebGPU too (`setBloomShareOnWebGPU` in `webgpu.ts`), and its first level at half the scene's pixels as WebGL's `UnrealBloomPass` draws it (`BLOOM_FIRST_LEVEL`): three's `BloomNode` was set to the scene's full size, so a phone's Very low drew its bright pass and first blur at 3120x1440, and each level was half as wide on the screen as WebGL's (`lab passes --ab --renderer webgpu`: 0.2 to 1% of the pixels, round the fires and glows). **A smaller bloom reads every pixel under it** (`BRIGHT_FROM_SCENE`, `brightTaps`): its bright pass's target is half bloom's size each way and a tap reads 2x2 of the scene's pixels, all of them at High, a quarter at Low and a sixteenth at Very low, so a small bright thing (a dummy's mark, a rune, a flame's tongue) was caught by one frame and missed by the next as the view moved and its whole glow went with it (from play, on main at Very low: "blinding light... even dummies emit it, but it only happens when I'm moving"). It takes 1, 2 or 4 taps a side, each thresholded alone as High's one is, and bloom's levels are weighed by their width on the screen (`bloomLevelsAt`: at half size a level is as wide as the next one up was). `lab light --levels --walk 90`: Very low's frame changed 0.46% a frame as the hero walked, 0.19% now, High's 0.18%. WebGL's alone so far: three's bloom node on WebGPU reads as it did. Anything gathered from the scene at a share of its size takes this care: a pass that reads fewer pixels than lie under it flickers as the view moves, and no still picture shows it.
- **Compiling programs** (a lit program's room on a phone, what a first visit's compile costs, warm-ups behind the lobby, ANGLE's compiles at a draw, the shadows' programs: `shaders.ts`, `game/warmup.ts`, `game/warm.ts`): read [`docs/agents/shaders.md`](../../docs/agents/shaders.md) before changing a material's program or a warm-up.
- **The floors' relief** (`core/parallax.ts`, parallax occlusion): a floor's height, kept in its roughness map's red channel (`buildPBR` in `core/pbr.ts`: no material reads it, roughness is green), is walked down along the view ray and its maps read where the ray meets it, so the crypt's grout and the forest's soil lie sunk below the cobbles' and pebbles' tops. Only the hollows sink: the tops stay at the floor's own level, where everything on the floor stands. It samples with the floor's own uv gradients (`textureGrad`: in a loop the GPU's are undefined, and the moved uv's jump at each stone's edge), takes up to `relief` steps (the quality preset's: 16, 8, none on Low; half of them looking straight down) and fades out by `fade` m (past the top-down view's 30 to 51). Its shift is a pixel or two from the top-down view and shows most over the shoulder and through the eyes. Apply it with `floorRelief(shader, ...)` after a floor's own edits in its `onBeforeCompile`. A floor has to give it something to lift: the forest's (`forestFloor` in `core/pbr.ts`, 5 m a tile, heights in cm) is a litter of fallen leaves in three layers (each turned its own way, cupped, with a raised midrib and veins, the lower ones older and darker), twigs and pebbles over clumpy soil, whatever lies highest showing at each point; as soil with flat round leaves 1 cm a texel, it showed no relief at all.
- **The views and the aim** (V's views, first person, what the cursor aims at and what the aim meets: `input.ts`, the cameras): read [`docs/agents/views.md`](../../docs/agents/views.md) before changing either.

- `input.ts`: a body's aim goes `AIM_INTO` in from where the eye's ray meets the foe, towards the foe's middle, not on along the ray: from the camera's height the ray is steep, and carried on along it a shot meeting a foe's side was aimed past it (Briarflight's middle arrow felled nothing, `lab death`).
