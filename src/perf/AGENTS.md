# `src/perf`

Performance reports a slow game sends with its player's consent, for agents to read and fix (`npm run lab -- trace`).

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); the service they go to is `server/traces/AGENTS.md`.

- `recorder.ts`: always on, from the boot to the page's end, in every scene (the boot, the hero selection screen, the lobby, a fight, a loading bar's wait). Each frame's times and counts go into a ring of columns (`RING`: about the last minute), beside the boot's steps, the long animation frames and marks (a scene changing, a wave, a biome, a hero built, a cast or channel of the local hero's, a loading bar's end, the window's size and full screen as they change: a phone's trace held up 212 ms in a resize's listeners, and nothing in it said the window had changed, trace Z8GY-RHSN). An update stage's column is named apart from the fixed columns (`stageColumn`: the stage `particles` took the particles' count's column, kind and all, and the report read 0.0999 particles; `lab recorder` checks every count's kind). `perfHud.ts` hands it every frame: its timings run whether the overlay shows or not.
- `detect.ts`: pure. Once a second the last stretch is judged: over 20 s of play (a paused game, the pause menu, a hidden tab, a warm-up, a loading bar and the 3 s after a scene starts left out), a 1% low under 30 frames a second (the display's jitter taken off its frame, never added: a steady 60 on an S25 Edge's 120 Hz screen drops a frame now and then, 33.4 ms, and read raw it was 29.9 a second and sent as slow, trace R2XY-H2DW; and none at all where it's no longer than 2.2 of the game's own frames, a refresh missed now and then: a steady 60 on a 60 Hz screen whose Chrome ticked at another screen's 145 Hz showed a frame for 5 ticks now and then, 34.7 ms, a low of 29, and was sent as slow, trace T9NG-CZ79, `ONE_MISSED`), or over a fifth of the display's frames missed with the mean under 50 (the display's rate is the fastest second seen); over every frame shown but a hidden tab's, stalls (one of a second or more, or three of 250 ms); a boot whose own work (its time less the time it waited on its files) took over 15 s; a loading bar up over 8 s.
- **A slow boot's trace waits for the play after it** (`BOOT_TAIL_MS`, 45 s, in `recorder.ts`): taken at the reveal, a laptop's held its last second alone, 17 frames, while its player saw 5 frames a second for a minute, and the session's one ask went on it (trace JGNM-1NCF). It's taken 45 s after the reveal with the detector's verdict on that play beside the boot's time (`Sent because: boot, the boot took 18.4 s; the play after it: ...`), anything else found slow meanwhile left to it. `lab recorder` checks it (its canary: taken at the reveal).
- `consent.ts`: the prompt (`#perf-ask`, `styles/_perfAsk.scss`) and what the player chose (`localStorage` `last-stand-perf-traces`; the pause menu's Slow-game reports: Ask, Always, Off).
- `upload.ts`: the snapshot packed with the device, the settings and the build, gzipped by the browser and posted to `<relay host>/traces`.
- `traceFormat.ts`: the packed format (pure, no imports: Node runs it as it is, the lab and the service's tests import it). `traceReport.ts`: a trace read as the perf overlay's report reads (pure: `lab trace`).

## Gotchas

- **Never during a fight, never blocking.** A slow stretch found is kept as a snapshot at once (only copied: 1.9 ms for a full minute in the lab's browser, `lab recorder --frames 3600`), and the player is asked once a calm moment has lasted 1.5 s: the choice after a wave (the prompt under it: both stand at the top middle, and the prompt hid its buttons), the summary, the lobby, the hero selection screen. A fight starting hides it, and it's shown again at the next calm. The packing and gzip wait for the calm too.
- **Asked at most once a session; three Not nows and it stops asking.** Always keeps sending without asking, 10 minutes apart. **However asked, at most 10 sends in any 24 hours** (`MOST_A_DAY`), each counted as it's tried and kept in `localStorage` across reloads; the traces server holds each address to the same. Off never snapshots anything (`recorder.wanted`: nothing is copied unless it could go somewhere). In co-op each game asks its own player about its own frames.
- **Nothing about the player**: no names, items, rooms, invite codes or address beyond the page's path and its render switches (`KEPT_SWITCHES`). The prompt says what's in it and its size.
- **Compact, no text repeated**: each column a byte or two a frame (a time as a step of 8% on a log scale from 0.05 ms, under it 0, each within 4%; the frame's interval to a fiftieth of a ms; counts as they are), split into byte planes so gzip finds their runs, and every mark's and long frame's text once in a string table. Real traces at 120 frames a second come to 40 to 44 KB a minute gzipped, about 5.8 B a frame. A trace over the 1 MB the service takes is still cut to its newest half until it fits (`packTrace`).
- **Tune the packing on real traces, not made-up values**: take one from a game in the installed Chrome with `npm run stalls -- --scenario <scene> --read "window.__traceNow()"` (`__traceNow` is in every build: the packed snapshot in base64) and read it with `npm run lab -- trace --file tools/stalls/out/read-N.json --sizes`: each part's weight, and the trace packed each other way the format can (`Packing`: the steps, the floor, columns as each frame's change) against its own. On four (a fight, the lobby, the hero screen, a stash sweep), 8% steps beat 4% by 11 to 17%, values as they are beat each frame's change by 6 to 8%, and 12% steps would save 10% more at 6% each. A trace whose header names no packing was packed the format's first way (`FIRST_PACKING`).
- **The service worker never sees an upload**: it answers GETs of the game's own files alone (`scripts/pwa.ts`), so a POST to another host passes it by, under any build's worker. The request is a plain one (no headers of its own, no cookies), so no preflight.
- **Measure it with `npm run lab -- recorder`**: the recorder's calls timed each frame in a fight (0.02 to 0.03 ms a frame on the owner's PC, against a budget of 0.05), the trace packed, sent to a traces service of the lab's own and read back with its token; `--canary` makes each frame's recording 0.2 ms slower, which it must catch. The prompt's states are `lab ui --states perfAsk,perfAskBreak,perfAskTouch`. The detector's and the format's unit tests are in `tools/lab/test/trace.test.mjs`.
- The lab's frames run back to back without yielding a task, and the GPU's timer queries come back only after one, so `lab recorder` sees few GPU times or none (`lab perf` yields after each frame for them): a player's paced frames have them.
