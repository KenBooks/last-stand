# `src/engine`

The Rust engine's trial: Bulwark, our own WebGPU-only renderer in Rust and WebAssembly, drawing the game's scene. **An experiment on the branch `experiment/rust-engine`, never to merge as it is.** `STATE.md` is the run's state file: what works, what was measured, what's next.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **The engine's source is the private repository `VictorZakharov/bulwark`** (locally `G:\Git\bulwark`); `pkg/` here is its build, checked in so a PR's preview has it: `bash scripts/build.sh` there, then copy `pkg/bulwark.js`, `bulwark.d.ts`, `bulwark_bg.wasm` and `bulwark_bg.wasm.d.ts` over. Never edit `pkg/` by hand. A binary in the repository breaks the root's rule on binary assets: one more reason this branch doesn't merge as it is.
- **The game keeps its three.js scene; the engine is handed it** (`bridge.ts`). Each frame the bridge walks what's shown, hands over each geometry, map and material the first time it's met (again when its version moves on), and writes a record a mesh (24 floats: ids, range, flags, matrix) straight into the engine's memory, with the skinned meshes' bones and the frame's globals. The engine culls, sorts alike things together, sends every object's row in one write and draws each run of alike things as one instanced call. A call into the engine that grows its memory moves it: the bridge takes its views again (`freshViews`) after any such call.
- **On this branch the plain address loads the engine** (`core/rendererChoice.ts`); `?renderer=webgpu` and `?renderer=webgl` load three's, to compare. The game still boots three's WebGPU renderer under the engine, for what the engine doesn't do yet (the warm-ups, the portraits, the overlays' canvas): only the frame's scene is the engine's, on a canvas of its own over three's (`index.ts`).
- **What the engine doesn't draw is counted, not hidden** (`Bridge.skipped`, `lab rust`'s "not drawn" line): shader materials, materials moved or cut by three's nodes with no look of their own (the flames, the sky, the portals' glow), points, lines, sprites.
- **A material brings its own look as WGSL** (`custom.ts`, `rustLook(material, look)`; each look's shader in `looks/`, beside the game's nodes it ports line for line): two functions the engine puts in its lit shader, `vertex_hook` (moves a vertex, hands the fragment three vec4s) and `surface_hook` (changes the surface before it's lit: colour, normal, roughness, how much it shines, how much sun comes through it in shadow; false drops the pixel), with 192 floats of its own (`m.c`, written each frame by `numbers`) and two maps read in either stage. The village's meadow is the first (`looks/meadow.ts`: its blades placed from their instance's cell, the sward under them), and matched three's frame the first time it drew. A geometry that counts its own instances (`InstancedBufferGeometry`: the meadow's blades) is one record with the count (`RAW_INSTANCES`), its look placing each from its index; its mesh's `onBeforeRender` is run first where the look asks (`before`: the meadow lists its tiles and sets its numbers there). Such a material is never shared, casts no shadow (the shadow's pass doesn't know where its vertices go) and isn't culled.
- **The looks ported so far** (`looks/`: each registered where the game makes the material, under `RUNNING.rust`): the meadow, the three skies (drawn on the far plane, `Vertex.far`, and out of the fog, `Surface.fog`), the portals' membrane and the light they spill, the Thornwood's moss, its grass in the wind (a tuft's place is `Vertex.origin`) and its shafts of light, and the fires' flames (their ray march is `fireNodes.ts`'s own WGSL, `flameWgsl`; their baked loop is the look's `volume`, read by `samp_clamp`; blended as light already multiplied by its alpha, the key's blend 3). Most of the game's node code already holds its WGSL (`wgslFn`): a port is its functions and a hook that calls them. Since: the crypt's damp floor, its dim outside and its beacons, the fires' embers, the dazed stars (sprites cut to a star), the foes' kit look (`looks/kit.ts`: dissolve, frost, char, hit flash, rim; found on a material by its `kitLooks`, and shared by its first 16 numbers so a kind's bodies still batch: 932 draws to 363 with 24 foes), the fountain's water and its floor's web of light (`looks/water.ts`). A look that leaves its vertices where they are says so (`casts`) and casts the sun's shadow. Left: a painted face's hair tint, a worn item's glow, the hood's shade and the shine's cap (the kit's), the water's share of the sky by the hour and its capped glints, the paving's unrepeated maps, each membrane and flame fading as its prop dissolves. **A mark at the top of the page says the engine is drawing** (`watermark` in `index.ts`: "BULWARK · Rust engine"; for now, from the owner: "so I know if its that, or our old one").
- **The game's own culls and casters are three's, and the engine has its own**: an instanced mesh culled cell by cell (`cullInCells`) is left whole under the engine, which culls an instance at a time in its view and the sun's (packed by cells in a hook the engine never runs, the Thornwood drew without its trees, a boulder and half its thicket for a day); a mesh whose material writes no colour is a caster for the shadow alone (`SHADOW_ONLY`: drawn, the hall of heroes stood under its merged caster, a flat white shell, from play "what happened to the house ?"). **Compare every new view with `--three` before calling it drawn**: both were plain in the first picture taken beside three's.
- **The frame is bloomed and graded as the game's is** (the engine's post.wgsl, after `core/renderer.ts`'s chain, its numbers kept: the bright pass from 0.9, five levels blurred as three's `UnrealBloomPass`, the eye's adaptation over a 16x9 and a 4x3 grid, the highlights' knee, three's ACES at the game's exposure, the vignette, cool shadows and warm highlights, grain, a blow's red, low health's grey). The scene is drawn as light into a half-float target; `render` hands the grade's numbers over as it sets them for three (`Grade` in `bridge.ts`). Not ported: the village's fog of what he can't see, the blood round the screen's edges, photo mode, HDR screens.
- **A frame is written down as a tape and replayed with no browser** (`tape.ts`, `Bridge.capture`, `npm run lab -- rust --village --capture 512`: everything handed over anew, its maps no larger than that a side, saved in `tools/lab/out/rust-tapes`): the engine's `replay` draws it natively, pictures it and times it in 2.5 s, and the engine builds in 2 s for it (`cargo build --profile harness --bin replay`), against a page's boot of 25 to 40 s. For work on the engine's shaders and passes; what needs the game to run still needs the page. A capture leaves the engine holding everything twice: a harness's page alone.
- **`lab rust` says what the engine and the GPU say while it draws** (its problems: `said while drawing`): a pass the GPU refuses draws a black frame and no error reaches the lab otherwise (a timestamp asked of a pass with neither end set did, for an hour). The engine's shaders are read and checked by `cargo test` in its repository, with no GPU.
- **A page the lab booted ahead holds the engine as it was built then**: after copying a new `pkg/`, the next command still runs the old one once in a while (the lab reboots on a change to the source, and the page's module is fetched once). Run the command twice, or `lab stop` first, before reading a result as the new build's.
- **three's own work for the scene is left out under it**: its warm-ups compile and draw nothing (`warmUpWebGPU` in `core/shaders.ts`, which still hides what a warm-up shows: left shown, every warm-up's samples stood in the scene), and what the game hangs on three's render of the scene isn't run (`RUST.hooks`: the world's culling by cells, the kept shadow maps' draws). With the hooks run the hero was drawn lying on his side, a state one of them leaves between its two halves.
- **The engine's GPU time comes back on the browser's own time** (a timestamp query mapped a few frames later): a measure that steps frames without letting the browser go reads one stale value for the whole run, and did, for the first table this PR showed. `lab rust` waits a real frame after each step.
- **The village's lamps are point lights to it** (`addLamps`: `core/lamps.ts`'s list, soft close to as the game's are), with no shadows: they shine through walls.
- **Look-alike materials share one engine material** (`sharedId`): each foe's pieces have materials of their own, and by their numbers and maps they're the same, so a kind's bodies batch (1,807 draws to 887 with 24 foes). One whose numbers keep moving gets its own after 8 moves.
- **Crowds are off under it** (`entities/models/crowd.ts`): a crowd's rows are read by a shader of three's, so each body is drawn piece by piece, and the engine batches what's alike.
- **Measure with `npm run lab -- rust`** (`tools/lab/page/rustEngine.ts`): the engine's frame pictured and timed where the hero stands (`--village --hour 12`, `--biome forest --foes 24`), `--three` the same stand on three's WebGPU renderer, `--census` the shown materials by what they ask of a renderer, `--tune rust.debug=1` the sun's reach alone (`TUNE` in `bridge.ts`).
