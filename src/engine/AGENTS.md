# `src/engine`

The Rust engine: Bulwark, our own WebGPU-only renderer in Rust and WebAssembly, which alone draws the game's scene (three's WebGL path and three's WebGPU drawing of the scene were taken out on this branch: nothing to fall back to, nothing to compare with but a commit, `--ab`). **An experiment on the branch `experiment/rust-engine`, never to merge as it is.** `STATE.md` is the run's state file: what works, what was measured, what's next.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **The engine's source is the private repository `VictorZakharov/bulwark`** (locally `G:\Git\bulwark`); `pkg/` here is its build, checked in so a PR's preview has it: `bash scripts/build.sh` there, then copy `pkg/bulwark.js`, `bulwark.d.ts`, `bulwark_bg.wasm` and `bulwark_bg.wasm.d.ts` over, and write the Bulwark commit's full id in `pkg/BUILT_FROM` (`npm run check:engine`, in the PR checks, fails a branch that changes the build without it). Never edit `pkg/` by hand. **Its diff is noise**: wasm-bindgen's names (`__wasm_bindgen_func_elem_1754`) are places in the wasm's function table, renumbered by every build, so `.gitattributes` marks `pkg/` generated (GitHub folds it, `git diff` says it differs) and never merges it: a conflict there (two branches each with a build) is settled by building Bulwark again at the commit that has both, never by taking either side's lines. A reviewer reads `BUILT_FROM` and Bulwark's log between the two ids. A binary in the repository breaks the root's rule on binary assets: one more reason this branch doesn't merge as it is.
- **The game keeps its three.js scene; the engine is handed it** (`bridge.ts`). Each frame the bridge walks what's shown, hands over each geometry, map and material the first time it's met (again when its version moves on), and writes a record a mesh (24 floats: ids, range, flags, matrix) straight into the engine's memory, with the skinned meshes' bones and the frame's globals. The engine culls, sorts alike things together, sends every object's row in one write and draws each run of alike things as one instanced call. A call into the engine that grows its memory moves it: the bridge takes its views again (`freshViews`) after any such call.
- **The engine alone draws the game** (`core/rendererChoice.ts`, which reads nothing from the address: no `?renderer=`, `&meshlets=1` or `&lights=each`): an engine that doesn't start fails the boot, and the loading screen says so, as it says a browser has no WebGPU (`ui/loading.ts`). The game still boots three's WebGPURenderer, as the engine's host, for what the engine doesn't do (the overlays' canvas: the labels and the outline); the guide's portraits are drawn by a WebGL renderer of their own. Only the frame's scene is the engine's, on a canvas of its own (`index.ts`).
- **What the engine doesn't draw is counted, not hidden** (`Bridge.skipped`, `lab rust`'s "not drawn" line): shader materials, materials moved or cut by three's nodes with no look of their own (the flames, the sky, the portals' glow), points, lines, sprites.
- **A material brings its own look as WGSL** (`custom.ts`, `rustLook(material, look)`; each look's shader in `looks/`, beside the game's nodes it ports line for line): two functions the engine puts in its lit shader, `vertex_hook` (moves a vertex, hands the fragment three vec4s) and `surface_hook` (changes the surface before it's lit: colour, normal, roughness, how much it shines, how much sun comes through it in shadow; false drops the pixel), with 192 floats of its own (`m.c`, written each frame by `numbers`) and two maps read in either stage. The village's meadow is the first (`looks/meadow.ts`: its blades placed from their instance's cell, the sward under them), and matched three's frame the first time it drew. A geometry that counts its own instances (`InstancedBufferGeometry`: the meadow's blades) is one record with the count (`RAW_INSTANCES`), its look placing each from its index; its mesh's `onBeforeRender` is run first where the look asks (`before`: the meadow lists its tiles and sets its numbers there). Such a material is never shared, casts no shadow (the shadow's pass doesn't know where its vertices go) and isn't culled.

- **A look's constant vector is a module `const`, never a `let` whose parts are read** (`let d = vec2f(0.83, 0.55);` then `d.x` or `d[0]`, a `let` of literals alone or of sums of them): the lit shader has a pipeline constant (`LAYER_PART`), so Firefox's WebGPU runs naga's pass over the constants on every stage, and naga 30 emits such a `let` twice and refuses the stage (`ExpressionAlreadyInScope`). Chrome compiles it, so the lab never sees it: the meadow blades' `downwind` left the village's grass blades undrawn in Firefox at every level (report V1T3-G00X; in a joined module it would take every look joined with it). A swizzle of two or more (`d.yx`), a `let` of a module `const`, and a scalar `let` pass. **Check a new or changed look with `npm run lab -- naga`**: every module the page made, through naga as Firefox takes each stage (`tools/lab/commands/naga.md`).
- **The ground's value noise is read from a table** (`looks/noiseTable.ts`: the paving's, the sward's and the blades'): one `textureGather` a value in place of four `fract(sin(...))` hashes, 32 a pixel of the sward and 20 of the paving, which a phone pays in full (the S25 at Very low: paving 1.57 ms and sward 1.51 of 22, SBW3-8M8S). Gathered, not filtered: the sampler weighs its blend in 256ths, steps the paving's choice of its stones' offsets would show. The pattern repeats every 256 cells and differs from the hashes' (a GPU's own rounding of `sin` made those differ from device to device anyway). On the 4070 Ti at 5760 by 3240 in the square: paving 0.68 ms to 0.64, sward 0.53 to 0.46; a desktop's `sin` is cheap, the phone's part is the one to read.
